# Harbourline Software Ltd — Employee Handbook 2026

## 1 Welcome and how to use this handbook

### 1.1 A note from the Chief Executive

Welcome to Harbourline. Whether you joined us last week or have been here since the days when the whole company fitted round two tables in a converted chandlery on Spike Island, this handbook is for you. We are now about 180 people, most of us in Bristol and around twenty in our office in Leeds, and we build scheduling, booking and field-service software for housing associations, local authorities and facilities companies across the UK and northern Europe.

Growing from a small team into a mid-sized company has meant writing down things we used to settle over a cup of tea. We have tried to keep the rules short, explain why each one exists, and leave room for judgement. Where a rule seems not to fit your situation, talk to your manager or to the People team before you work around it. We would rather fix a rule than have people quietly ignore it.

Fiona Achterberg, Chief Executive

### 1.2 What this handbook covers

This handbook sets out how we work together: hours, pay, leave, travel, equipment, conduct, learning and what happens when someone leaves. It applies to everyone employed directly by Harbourline Software Ltd, whether full-time or part-time, permanent or on a fixed-term contract. Contractors and agency workers follow the terms of their own agreements, although the sections on IT security (section 8), health and safety (section 9) and conduct (section 10) apply to them while they work with us.

The handbook is version 2026.1.[^1] It replaces all earlier versions, including the separate travel guide and the IT acceptable use statement that used to sit on the intranet as stand-alone documents.

### 1.3 Contractual and non-contractual parts

Your employment contract and your offer letter are the documents that set out your legal terms. Some parts of this handbook are incorporated into your contract and are marked in the text as contractual: the notice rules in 12.2 and 12.3, the annual leave entitlement in 6.1 and 6.2, and the company sick pay scheme in 7.2. Everything else is company policy. Policies are not contractual, which means we can update them after consulting the Employee Forum (see 10.9), and we will tell you at least four weeks before any change takes effect unless the change is required by law sooner.

If anything in this handbook conflicts with your individual contract, your contract wins.

### 1.4 How to find what you need

The handbook is organised by topic rather than by department. A few tips:

- Section 13 lists who to contact about what, with email addresses and phone numbers for both offices.

If you cannot find an answer here, ask in the #ask-people channel. Questions asked there are answered within two working days, and frequent questions are added to the next version of this handbook.

## 2 Starting with us

### 2.1 Your offer and contract

Every new employee receives a written offer letter and a contract of employment before their first day. The contract states your job title, your manager's role, your salary, your normal place of work (Bristol or Leeds), your working hours, your holiday entitlement and the notice periods that apply during and after probation. Please sign and return the contract through the e-signature link within ten days of receiving it. If you have questions about any clause, the recruiter who made your offer will put you in touch with the right person in the People team.

Most roles are permanent. We also offer fixed-term contracts for project work and for cover during long absences such as maternity leave. Fixed-term employees have the same benefits as permanent staff, pro rata where relevant, and are told about permanent vacancies in the same way.

### 2.2 Conditions of your offer

Offers are conditional on the following checks, all of which must be complete before or on your first day:

1. Proof of your right to work in the UK.[^2] We check original documents or use a certified digital identity provider; for British and Irish citizens with a valid passport this usually takes a few minutes during your onboarding call.
2. Two satisfactory references (see 2.3).
3. For roles that give access to client systems in the public sector, a basic criminal record check through the Disclosure and Barring Service. Your recruiter will tell you if your role needs one. We pay the fee.
4. Confirmation of any professional qualification that the role requires, such as a cloud certification for platform engineers.

If a check cannot be completed in time, we may move your start date rather than withdraw the offer. We only withdraw an offer where a check reveals something that genuinely affects your ability to do the job, and we will always talk to you first.

### 2.3 References

We ask for two referees. One should be your current or most recent employer, normally a line manager or the HR team. The second can be a former employer, a university tutor or, for people entering work for the first time, someone who has supervised you in a volunteer or part-time role. Family members cannot act as referees.

We do not contact your current employer until you have accepted our offer and told us we may do so. If you would rather your employer were not contacted until after you have resigned, tell your recruiter and we will make the start of employment conditional on that reference instead.

When Harbourline gives a reference for a former employee, we confirm job title, dates of employment and, if asked, salary on leaving. Managers must not give personal references on company letterhead or from a company email address; see 12.8.

### 2.4 Your first week

Your first day starts at 10:00 so that you avoid the rush hour and your manager has time to set up. In Bristol you will be met in reception at Harbourline House; in Leeds, the site lead, Priya Ramdhanie, or one of her team will meet you. Remote starters receive their equipment by courier at least two working days before they begin and join a video welcome at 10:00.

During the first week you will:

- complete IT setup with the service desk, including your security key and password manager (see 8.4);
- meet your buddy, an experienced colleague from another team who is your first point of call for everyday questions;
- attend the "How Harbourline works" session, held every second Monday, which covers our products, clients and history;
- complete the mandatory e-learning modules on information security, data protection, health and safety, and equality and inclusion;
- agree a 30-60-90 day plan with your manager.

Engineers also pair with a member of their squad on a small, real change in the first fortnight. We find that shipping something early is the quickest way to learn our codebase and release process.

### 2.5 Probation

All new employees serve a probation period of six months. Probation gives both you and Harbourline a structured chance to check that the role suits you. It is not a test to be passed in silence: your manager should give you feedback throughout, and you should raise anything that is getting in your way.

The probation process has three formal points:

- **Month one check-in.** A short conversation about how onboarding is going, whether you have the access and equipment you need, and whether the 30-60-90 plan still makes sense.
- **Mid-point review at three months.** A written review against the plan, with clear notes on anything that needs to improve.
- **Final review in month six.** Your manager confirms your appointment in writing, or, rarely, extends probation.

Probation can be extended once, by up to three months, if there has been a long absence or if specific concerns need more time. An extension must be agreed with the People team and confirmed to you in writing before the original probation ends, along with the reasons and what you need to do.

While you are on probation, the contract can be ended at short notice: you or the company need give only one week. This shorter period applies whether the decision is yours or ours, and the longer notice terms described in 12.2 begin only once your appointment has been confirmed. Company sick pay during probation is also more limited (see 7.2).

### 2.6 Internal transfers and promoted roles

People who move into a new role within Harbourline do not serve a second probation. Instead they have a three-month settling-in review with their new manager. If the move does not work out, we will try to find a suitable role at the previous level, which may not be the exact role you left.

### 2.7 Personal details

Please keep your home address, bank details, emergency contacts and any change of name up to date in the HR system, Peoplebase. Payroll uses the bank details held there on the 15th of each month, so changes made after that date take effect the following month. We hold employee records in line with data protection law and our employee data notice, which explains what we keep, why, and for how long.

## 3 Working hours, flexible and remote work

### 3.1 Normal working hours

The standard full-time working week at Harbourline is 37.5 hours, normally worked Monday to Friday. Our core hours are 10:00 to 16:00, with a lunch break of your choosing. Outside core hours you can arrange your day as suits you and your team, as long as you are reachable during core hours and meetings are scheduled inside them.

Some teams need cover outside these hours. Client Support runs a rota between 08:00 and 18:00 on weekdays, and the Platform team runs an on-call rota out of hours (see 3.6). If your role involves a rota, your contract and your team's working agreement say so.

### 3.2 Team working agreements

Each team writes a short working agreement covering when meetings happen, how quickly people are expected to reply to messages, and how holiday cover works. Agreements are reviewed every six months or when a team changes shape. They cannot override this handbook, but they can be stricter: for example, the Release team does not hold meetings on Wednesdays, which is our main release day.

### 3.3 Hybrid working

Harbourline is a hybrid company. Most people spend part of the week in one of our offices and part at home. The arrangement is:

- **Bristol.** Teams come into Harbourline House on two anchor days each week. For most teams these are Tuesday and Thursday; product and design teams use Monday and Wednesday. The rest of the week you may work from home or from the office.
- **Leeds.** The Leeds office is smaller and open Monday to Thursday. Leeds colleagues come in on at least one agreed day per week, usually Wednesday, when the Bristol leadership team often visits.
- **Remote contracts.** Around thirty employees have a home-based contract and live too far from either office to commute. They are expected to attend team events in person roughly once a quarter, with travel paid under section 5.

Anchor days are there for collaboration, onboarding and the conversations that work better face to face. If you cannot come in on an anchor day, let your manager know; occasional changes are fine and do not need a formal request.

### 3.4 Requesting flexible working

You may ask for a change to your hours, the times you work or where you work from your first day of employment. Common requests include part-time hours, compressed hours (for example, a full week worked over four and a half days), a nine-day fortnight, or different start and finish times for school runs or caring responsibilities.

To make a request, complete the flexible working form in Peoplebase. Your manager will meet you within three weeks to talk it through, and you will receive a decision in writing within two months of the request, including the time for any appeal. We approve the large majority of requests, sometimes after a trial period of eight to twelve weeks. Where we cannot agree, we will explain the business reason, suggest alternatives and tell you how to appeal. You may make two requests in any twelve-month period.

### 3.5 Working from abroad

Working temporarily from outside the UK raises tax, insurance and data protection questions, so it needs approval in advance. You may work from another country for up to 20 working days in a calendar year, in blocks of no more than ten working days, provided that:

- the country is on the approved list on the intranet, which currently covers the UK's neighbours in the European Economic Area, Switzerland and a few others;
- your manager and the People team have approved the dates at least three weeks beforehand;
- you connect only through the company VPN and keep to the rules on public networks in 8.6;
- you are available during UK core hours.

Working from abroad is separate from annual leave. If you add some working days to a holiday, the holiday days and working days must be recorded separately in Peoplebase.

### 3.6 On-call and out-of-hours work

Engineers in the Platform and Integrations teams take part in an on-call rota, usually one week in every six. While on call you must be able to respond within 30 minutes and have your laptop and a working internet connection with you. Each week on call is paid at an allowance of £150, and any time actually spent responding to incidents between 19:00 and 08:00 or at weekends is given back as time off in lieu (TOIL) at time and a half. TOIL should be taken within two months.

Outside on-call duties, we do not expect anyone to answer messages in the evening or at weekends. Use scheduled sending in email and chat if you work unusual hours, so that colleagues are not prompted to reply at 22:00.

### 3.7 Overtime and the Working Time Regulations

Salaried employees are not normally paid overtime. If your workload regularly takes you over your contracted hours, raise it with your manager; it is a sign that something needs to change. Under the Working Time Regulations your average working week should not exceed 48 hours over a 17-week reference period. You can choose to opt out of this limit by signing a written notice, and you can withdraw an opt-out with three months' notice, but we do not ask anyone to opt out and you will never be treated less favourably for refusing.

### 3.8 Recording time

Most employees do not record their hours. Client project teams record time against project codes in Timesheet each Friday, because many of our implementation contracts are billed by the day. Time recorded for billing is not used to monitor individual performance.

## 4 Pay, pensions and benefits

### 4.1 Salary and pay day

Salaries are paid monthly, in arrears, by bank transfer. Pay day is the 25th of each month. When the 25th falls on a Saturday, Sunday or bank holiday, you will be paid on the last working day before it; in December, pay day is brought forward to the last working day before Christmas so that nobody waits over the holiday. Your payslip is available in Peoplebase three working days before pay day.

Your first salary payment covers the days you have worked from your start date. If you join after the payroll cut-off on the 15th, your first month's pay is added to the following month's payment, and you can ask Payroll for an advance of up to 75% of the amount due if waiting would cause difficulty.

### 4.2 Salary bands and reviews

Every role sits in a salary band linked to our career framework (see 11.4). Bands are published on the intranet so that everyone can see the range for each level. We benchmark bands every autumn against salary data for software companies in Bristol, Leeds and the wider UK market.

Salary reviews take place once a year, with any increase effective from 1 April. The review considers market movement, your progress within the band, and the outcome of your annual development conversation. Promotions decided in the March and September rounds carry their own salary change, effective from the first day of the following month. We do not negotiate salaries outside these cycles except where someone takes on a substantially different role.

### 4.3 Deductions and errors

We make the deductions required by law, such as income tax, National Insurance and student loan repayments, plus any deductions you have agreed to in writing, such as pension contributions or the season ticket loan in 4.9. If you have been overpaid, Payroll will contact you before recovering the money and will agree a repayment plan; we will not recover more than 10% of your net monthly pay in one month unless you prefer to repay faster. If you have been underpaid because of our error, we will correct it by bank transfer within five working days of the error being confirmed rather than waiting for the next pay day.

### 4.4 Pension

All employees are enrolled in the Harbourline Group Personal Pension, run by Westgate Pension Trust, from their first day of employment, regardless of age or earnings.[^3] Contributions are made through salary sacrifice, which reduces the National Insurance that both you and the company pay.

You may change your contribution rate twice a year, in April and October, or at any time after a major life event such as the birth of a child. You can opt out of the scheme, but you will lose the employer contribution, and the law requires us to re-enrol you every three years. Before deciding, you may want to use the free sessions with an independent financial adviser that Westgate offers each spring; Harbourline cannot advise you on pension decisions.

If you earn above the HMRC annual allowance thresholds, you can ask for part of the employer contribution to be paid as a taxable cash supplement instead. Speak to Payroll for details.

### 4.5 Life assurance and income protection

From your first day, you are covered by group life assurance worth four times your basic salary. The benefit is paid to the people you name on your expression-of-wish form in Peoplebase, which you can update at any time. Please keep it current; trustees take the form into account, although they are not bound by it.

After you have completed probation, you are also covered by group income protection. If you are unable to work because of long-term illness or injury and your company sick pay has ended (see 7.2), the insurer pays 60% of your basic salary, less any state benefits, until you are able to return, retire or reach state pension age. The deferred period is 26 weeks.

### 4.6 Private medical insurance

Employees who have passed probation can join our private medical insurance scheme with Clearwater Health. Harbourline pays the premium for you; you can add a partner or children at your own cost through payroll. The scheme covers diagnosis and treatment of acute conditions, outpatient consultations up to £1,500 a year, mental health treatment and a virtual GP service available at any hour. It is a taxable benefit, so it appears on your P11D and may change your tax code.

### 4.7 Health cash plan and eye tests

Every employee is a member of the health cash plan from day one. It refunds part of the cost of routine dental treatment, optical care, physiotherapy and some therapies up to annual limits, which are listed in the plan booklet. Harbourline also pays for an eye test every two years for anyone who uses a screen as a significant part of their work, and contributes up to £75 towards glasses if the optician confirms they are needed specifically for screen use.

### 4.8 Cycle to work and commuting

Our cycle to work scheme lets you hire a bike and safety equipment up to £2,500 through salary sacrifice, spread over twelve months. Both offices have secure bike storage and showers. In Bristol the bike store is in the courtyard, accessed with your building pass; in Leeds it is in the basement of the building next door, by arrangement with the landlord.

Parking at Harbourline House is limited to six spaces, which are reserved for colleagues with a blue badge, for visitors and for people who need a car for client site visits on that day. Book a space through the facilities page on the intranet.

### 4.9 Season ticket loan

If you commute by train or bus, you can borrow up to £5,000 interest-free to buy an annual season ticket. The loan is repaid in equal monthly deductions from your salary over the life of the ticket. If you leave before the loan is repaid, the balance is deducted from your final pay, and if your final pay is not enough, we will agree how you will pay the rest.

### 4.10 Other benefits

- **Employee Assistance Programme.** Free, confidential support by phone, online chat or in person, available every hour of the day, covering money worries, legal questions, family matters and short courses of counselling. See 9.7.
- **Wellbeing allowance.** £300 a year to spend on something that supports your physical or mental wellbeing, such as a gym membership, a yoga course, swimming lessons or therapy sessions. Claim it through the expenses system with a receipt.
- **Home office allowance.** A one-off £250 when you start, for desk accessories or lighting at home, plus the equipment in section 8.
- **Volunteering days.** Two paid days a year to volunteer for a registered charity or community organisation of your choice (see 7.11).
- **Birthday.** An extra day off in the week of your birthday, to be taken within that week or lost.
- **Discounts.** Access to the discount platform run by our benefits provider, Brightwell Rewards, with savings at supermarkets, cinemas and high street shops.
- **Team socials.** Each team has a social budget of £40 per person per quarter, plus the whole-company summer and winter events.

Benefits that are taxable are reported to HMRC on form P11D or through payroll. The benefits booklet in Peoplebase sets out the tax position of each one.

### 4.11 Referral bonus

If you refer someone who is hired into a permanent role, you receive a bonus of £1,500, paid in two instalments: £500 when they start and £1,000 when they pass probation. Referrals for hard-to-fill roles, marked on the careers page, earn £2,500. Members of the hiring panel for the role cannot receive a referral bonus for it, and neither can the hiring manager.

## 5 Expenses and business travel

### 5.1 Principles

We pay the reasonable costs you incur when working for Harbourline, so that nobody is out of pocket. In return, we ask you to spend company money as carefully as you would spend your own, pick the cheaper route when it serves the purpose equally well, and keep receipts. Everything in this section follows HMRC rules on business expenses, which means most legitimate claims are paid free of tax.

### 5.2 What counts as a business expense

Typical business expenses include:

- travel to a client site, partner office or event that is not your normal place of work;
- travel between the Bristol and Leeds offices;
- hotels and meals when you are away overnight;
- registration fees for events approved in advance by your manager;
- small purchases of materials for a workshop, client demonstration or team event;
- mobile data or roaming charges incurred while working abroad on approved trips.

Your ordinary commute between home and your normal office is not a business expense. For employees with a home-based contract, the office they travel to for team events counts as a temporary workplace, so those journeys can be claimed.

### 5.3 Claiming expenses

Claims are submitted through the expenses app, Ledgerly, on your phone or laptop. Photograph each receipt as soon as you get it; the app reads the date, supplier and amount, and you add the purpose and project code. Claims should be submitted within 60 days of the expense. Claims older than 90 days are only paid in exceptional circumstances with the Finance Director's approval.

Your manager approves your claim in Ledgerly, and Finance checks it against this policy. Once a claim has been approved by the 15th of the month, the money is paid into your bank account together with that month's salary. Claims approved after the 15th are added to the payroll run in the month after. If a large expense would leave you short before then, ask Finance for an advance or use a company card.

### 5.4 Company cards

Employees who travel more than about once a month, and managers who regularly pay for team events, can apply for a company credit card. Cards have a monthly limit of £2,000, which can be raised for individual trips with Finance approval. You must still upload a receipt for every transaction within 14 days. Personal spending on a company card is not allowed, even if you intend to repay it; if it happens by mistake, tell Finance straight away and the amount will be deducted from your next salary.

### 5.5 Booking travel

All rail, air and hotel bookings for business travel should be made through the Travelwise portal, which applies our policy automatically and keeps a record of where travellers are in case of emergency. You can book outside the portal only if the same journey or room is at least 15% cheaper elsewhere, or if the portal cannot offer what you need; in that case, keep a screenshot of the portal price with your claim.

Book as early as you reasonably can. For rail travel between Bristol and Leeds, booking two weeks ahead typically halves the fare.

### 5.6 Rail, air and local transport

- **Rail.** Standard class is the default. You may travel first class on journeys longer than three hours if the first-class ticket costs no more than £30 above the standard fare, or if you need the space for health reasons agreed with the People team.
- **Air.** Economy class for all flights. Flights within Great Britain are only booked when the train would take more than five hours each way or the flight is cheaper overall after counting a hotel night. Checked baggage is allowed when you carry demonstration equipment or are away for more than three nights.
- **Taxis.** Use public transport where it is practical. Taxis are fine late at night, when you are carrying equipment, when travelling between a station and a site with no regular bus, or when several colleagues travelling together make a taxi cheaper than separate train tickets.
- **Car hire.** Through Travelwise only, in the smallest suitable category, with the company's insurance. Fill the tank before returning the car.

### 5.7 Using your own car

If you drive your own car on company business, you can claim mileage at the HMRC approved rates.[^4] You must have business use cover on your insurance and a valid MOT, and you should not drive for more than two hours without a break. Claim the extra mileage beyond your normal commute only: a Bristol employee who drives from home to a client in Swindon claims the full return distance only if it is longer than their usual round trip to Harbourline House, and otherwise claims the difference.

Passengers who are colleagues travelling on the same business trip can be claimed at 5p per mile each. Parking, tolls and congestion charges incurred on business journeys can be claimed with receipts. Parking fines and speeding penalties cannot.

Claim what you actually spend, up to the allowance, with receipts; the allowance is a ceiling, not a payment you receive automatically. ### 5.9 Staying with friends or family

If you stay with friends or family instead of in a hotel, you can claim a flat £30 per night towards their hospitality, without a receipt. You can still claim the meal allowance for meals you buy.

### 5.10 Client entertainment and gifts

Client entertainment, such as a dinner after a successful go-live, must be approved in advance by a director. The guide amount is £60 per head including drinks. Record the names of everyone present and their organisations on the claim. Many of our public sector clients have strict rules on accepting hospitality, so check with the client contact first; see 10.4 for the rules on gifts and hospitality offered to you.

### 5.11 Travel safety and insurance

Our business travel insurance covers you for medical treatment, cancellations, lost luggage and emergency assistance on all trips booked through Travelwise, including any personal days added to a business trip of up to three days. Download the insurer's app before travelling abroad; it holds the emergency number and the policy reference.

Before travelling outside the UK, check the government's travel advice for your destination and tell the Travel desk about any health condition that might need support. We do not send employees to destinations where official advice is against all but essential travel. If you feel unsafe on a trip, leave and come home; we will pay the extra cost without question.

### 5.12 Expenses we do not pay

To avoid misunderstandings, the following are not reimbursed: personal items such as toiletries or clothing (unless your luggage has been lost and the insurer has not yet paid out); in-room films and minibar items; fines of any kind; costs of a partner or friend who travels with you; upgrades to business or first class that are not allowed under 5.6; and tips above about 10% of the bill.

## 6 Annual leave and bank holidays

### 6.1 Your holiday entitlement

The holiday year runs from 1 January to 31 December. Full-time employees start with 25 days of paid annual leave each year. In addition, everyone has the bank holidays observed where they work, which in England are normally eight days a year. Holiday is contractual and is well above the statutory minimum.[^5]

Part-time employees receive a pro rata entitlement based on the number of hours they work. For example, someone working three days a week has 15 days of annual leave plus a proportion of bank holidays calculated in hours, so that part-time colleagues are not disadvantaged by which weekdays they work. Peoplebase shows your entitlement in hours as well as days.

If you start or leave part-way through the year, your entitlement is calculated in proportion to the whole months you work, rounded up to the nearest half day.

### 6.2 Extra days with length of service

Your annual leave increases as you stay with us. The extra days are added on 1 January following the anniversary that triggers them.

### 6.3 Booking holiday

Book holiday through Peoplebase. Give notice of at least twice the length of the leave you are requesting, so a week off needs two weeks' notice. Requests of more than ten consecutive working days need at least six weeks' notice and should be discussed with your manager before you book anything that cannot be refunded.

Managers should respond to holiday requests within five working days. They can refuse a request only where it would leave the team unable to meet a firm commitment, and they should suggest alternative dates. Approved holiday will not be cancelled except in a genuine emergency; if that happens, Harbourline will pay any non-refundable costs you have incurred.

### 6.4 Busy periods and the winter close-down

Harbourline closes between Christmas and New Year. The three working days between the Boxing Day bank holiday and New Year's Day are given as extra company days, not taken from your 25-day allowance. Client Support and the on-call rota keep a reduced service during this period, and anyone who works on those days receives the same number of days off to take in January or February.

Some teams have periods when holiday is restricted, which are agreed at the start of each year and listed in the team's working agreement. The Implementation team, for example, does not normally approve leave in the last two weeks of March, when many local authority clients go live before their financial year end.

### 6.5 Carrying holiday over

We want you to take your holiday. You may carry up to five unused days into the next holiday year, and they must be used by 31 March. Further days can be carried over only:

- if you could not take them because of sickness, maternity, paternity, adoption or other family leave, in which case statutory rules allow you to carry them over for up to 18 months;
- with the written approval of a director, in exceptional business circumstances.

Holiday cannot be exchanged for pay, except when you leave Harbourline (see 12.6).

### 6.6 Bank holidays

England's bank holidays apply in both offices. Employees with a home-based contract in Scotland or Northern Ireland follow the bank holidays of their nation instead, which keeps the number of days roughly equal.

If your role requires you to work on a bank holiday, for example on the on-call rota, you receive a day off in lieu in addition to the on-call allowance. You can also choose to swap a bank holiday for a day of personal or religious significance, by agreement with your manager, as long as you work on the bank holiday itself.

### 6.7 Buying and selling holiday

Once a year, in the November benefits window, you can buy up to five extra days of holiday for the coming year. The cost is deducted from your salary in equal instalments across the year. You can also sell up to three days, provided you still take at least 25 days in total including bank holidays, so that you remain above the statutory minimum.

### 6.8 Holiday and sickness

If you fall ill while on holiday, tell your manager on the first day of illness and follow the rules in 7.1. The days you are ill will then be treated as sick leave, and the holiday days can be taken again later. You will need a fit note or a medical certificate from a doctor in the country you are visiting for illness of more than seven days.

### 6.9 Long service sabbatical

After ten years of continuous service, you may take a paid sabbatical of four weeks in addition to your annual leave. It should be taken within two years of your tenth anniversary and planned with your manager at least three months ahead. Some colleagues combine it with annual leave or unpaid leave to travel, study or complete a personal project.

## 7 Sickness and other absence

### 7.1 If you are ill

If you are too unwell to work, tell your manager as early as possible and no later than 09:30 on your first day of absence. A phone call or a direct message is best; if you cannot reach your manager, contact their deputy or the People team. Please say roughly how long you expect to be off and whether any urgent work needs to be picked up. You do not need to give medical details beyond what helps us support you.

Then record the absence in Peoplebase on the day you return, or ask your manager to record it for you if you will be away for more than a few days. Keep your manager informed at intervals you agree together; for most short illnesses that means a quick message each morning.

For absences of up to seven calendar days, you complete a self-certification form in Peoplebase when you come back. From the eighth calendar day onwards you need a fit note from your GP, a hospital doctor or another qualified health professional. Fit notes can be photographed and uploaded to Peoplebase; you do not need to post the original.

If you feel unwell but able to work from home, you may do so with your manager's agreement, but please rest if you need to. Working while ill usually lengthens the illness.

### 7.2 Company sick pay

Harbourline pays company sick pay on top of Statutory Sick Pay,[^6] so that most people receive their normal pay during short absences. The amount depends on your length of service at the start of the absence and is counted over a rolling twelve-month period.

| Service at start of absence | Full pay | Half pay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| During probation | 5 working days | None |
| Probation completed, less than 3 years | 6 weeks | 6 weeks |
| 3 years or more | 12 weeks | 12 weeks |

After company sick pay ends, you receive Statutory Sick Pay for as long as you are eligible, and income protection may begin after 26 weeks of absence (see 4.5). Company sick pay is contractual but depends on your following the reporting rules in 7.1. If you do not tell us you are ill, or if we have good reason to believe an absence is not genuine, we may withhold company sick pay after discussing it with you.

### 7.3 Returning to work

After any absence of more than three working days, your manager will hold a short return-to-work conversation, usually on your first day back. The purpose is to welcome you back, check you are well enough to work, and agree any support you need, not to question whether you were really ill.

Where a fit note says you "may be fit for work" with adjustments, such as reduced hours, lighter duties or working from home, we will try to put those adjustments in place. A phased return after a long absence typically lasts two to six weeks, starting at around half your normal hours, with full pay throughout.

### 7.4 Long-term and frequent absence

If you are off sick for more than four weeks, or have had several short absences in a few months, your manager and a People Partner will meet you to understand what is happening and how we can help. With your consent, we may refer you to our occupational health provider, Meridian Occupational Health, for advice on adjustments and likely recovery time. You can bring a colleague or trade union representative to these meetings.

We treat absence linked to disability, pregnancy or gender reassignment separately from other absence, and we will always consider reasonable adjustments before taking any formal steps. Formal absence management under the capability procedure is a last resort and is described in the separate capability policy on the intranet.

### 7.5 Medical and dental appointments

Try to arrange routine appointments outside core hours or at the start or end of the day. Where this is not possible, take the time you need and make it up later by agreement with your manager. Hospital appointments, cancer screening, fertility treatment and appointments related to a disability or long-term condition are paid time off and do not need to be made up. Pregnant employees and their partners have a right to paid time off for antenatal appointments.

### 7.6 Maternity and adoption leave

Employees who are pregnant can take up to 52 weeks of maternity leave, whatever their length of service. Employees adopting a child, or having a child through surrogacy as intended parents, can take the same amount of adoption leave. Please tell the People team about a pregnancy by the end of the 15th week before the expected week of childbirth, or within seven days of being matched with a child for adoption. We will then send you a personal letter with your dates and pay.

Harbourline's enhanced pay applies to employees with at least six months' service at the 15th week before the expected week of childbirth or the week of matching:

- 16 weeks at full pay;
- the next 23 weeks at Statutory Maternity Pay or Statutory Adoption Pay;
- the remaining 13 weeks unpaid.

Enhanced pay is paid on condition that you return to work for at least three months after the leave. If you do not return, we may ask you to repay the enhanced part, although we waive this where the reason relates to your health or your child's health.

During maternity or adoption leave you can work up to ten "keeping in touch" days without ending your leave, paid at your normal daily rate. These are voluntary for both sides. Your holiday continues to build up throughout the leave, and pension contributions continue on the basis of your full salary for the paid weeks.

### 7.7 Paternity and partner leave

The partner of someone giving birth or adopting, of any gender, can take up to four weeks of paternity leave at full pay, provided they have completed probation. The first two weeks follow the statutory rules and can be taken as one block or two separate weeks at any time in the first year. The extra two weeks must be taken within six months of the birth or placement. Employees with less than six months' service receive Statutory Paternity Pay for the two statutory weeks.

### 7.8 Neonatal care, parental leave and time off for dependants

- **Neonatal care leave.** If your baby is admitted to neonatal care within 28 days of birth and stays for at least seven continuous days, you can take one week of neonatal care leave for each week of care, up to twelve weeks, paid at full salary. This is in addition to maternity, adoption or paternity leave.
- **Unpaid parental leave.** Parents with one year's service can take up to 18 weeks of unpaid parental leave for each child before the child's 18th birthday, in blocks of whole weeks and up to four weeks a year per child.
- **Time off for dependants.** You can take reasonable time off to deal with an emergency involving someone who depends on you, such as a child's sudden illness or a breakdown in care arrangements. Harbourline pays up to five days a year; further days are unpaid or can be taken as holiday.
- **Carer's leave.** If you care for someone with a long-term need, you can take up to one week of carer's leave a year, in half days or whole days, at full pay.

### 7.9 Compassionate and bereavement leave

When someone close to you dies, take the time you need. As a guide, Harbourline gives up to ten working days of paid leave on the death of a partner, child, parent or sibling, and up to three days for other relatives or close friends, including time to attend the funeral. Managers can and do give more where it is needed, for example where you are responsible for arranging the funeral or the estate, or where the funeral is abroad.

Parents who lose a child under the age of 18, or who suffer a stillbirth after 24 weeks of pregnancy, are entitled to two weeks of parental bereavement leave in addition to the leave above, at full pay. Employees who experience a pregnancy loss before 24 weeks, including partners, can take up to five days of paid leave without needing to call it sickness.

Compassionate leave is not limited to bereavement. If a close family member becomes seriously ill, speak to your manager about paid or unpaid leave and temporary flexible working.

### 7.10 Jury service and public duties

If you are summoned for jury service, tell your manager and send a copy of the summons to the People team as soon as you receive it. We will only ask the court to defer your service in exceptional circumstances, such as a critical client launch that nobody else can cover, and never more than once.

Harbourline continues to pay your full salary for up to ten working days of jury service. Before the trial ends, ask the court for a certificate of loss of earnings; you then claim the court's loss-of-earnings allowance, and Payroll deducts that amount from your salary, so that overall you are no worse off. If a trial lasts longer than ten working days, talk to the People team, who will arrange to continue paying your salary for the longer trial in the same way. If you are released early on any day, you are not expected to come back to work that day.

We also give reasonable paid time off, up to ten days a year, for public duties such as serving as a magistrate, a local councillor, a school governor or a member of a tribunal. Members of the reserve forces receive two weeks of paid leave a year for training, on top of annual leave.

### 7.11 Volunteering days

Each employee has two paid volunteering days a year (see 4.10). Agree the dates with your manager in the usual way and record them in Peoplebase as "volunteering". The days can be used for individual volunteering or for the organised team days run with our charity partners, such as the code clubs we support in Bristol primary schools and the digital skills sessions at Leeds Central Library.

### 7.12 Unpaid leave and career breaks

You can ask for unpaid leave of up to four weeks for any reason, such as an extended trip, a religious pilgrimage or a family commitment. Requests are approved by your manager and the People team, taking account of the needs of the team. Longer career breaks of three to twelve months are available to employees with three years' service; we guarantee a return to a role at the same level, though not necessarily the same role. Pension contributions and most insured benefits pause during unpaid leave of more than four weeks, so check the details with the People team before deciding.

### 7.13 Religious observance

We will try to accommodate requests for time off for religious festivals, using annual leave, unpaid leave or a bank holiday swap (see 6.6). During periods of fasting, you can agree adjusted working hours with your manager. Each office has a quiet room that can be booked for prayer, reflection or a break; in Bristol it is on the ground floor beside reception, and in Leeds it is the small meeting room called Kirkstall.

Harbourline provides everything you need to do your job, both in the office and at home. Equipment is ordered by your manager before you start and set up by the IT service desk, so that it is ready on your first day. The standard kit depends on your role:

### 8.2 Replacement and repairs

Company laptops are replaced every four years, or sooner if a repair would cost more than half the price of a new device. The service desk tracks the age of every laptop and will contact you about two months before your replacement is due, so that you can back up anything stored locally and arrange a convenient time for the swap.

Phones are on a shorter cycle: the service desk exchanges each handset for a current model once it has been in use for two years, because older phones stop receiving security updates sooner. Monitors, docks, headsets and chairs are not swapped on a timetable at all. They are kept in use for as long as they work properly, and you can request a replacement through the self-service portal whenever one becomes faulty or no longer meets your needs.

If something breaks, report it to the service desk straight away rather than trying to repair it yourself or taking it to a high street repair shop. Accidental damage is covered by our insurance and you will not be charged for it. Repeated damage caused by carelessness will be discussed with your manager.

### 8.3 Adjustments and specialist equipment

If you need equipment because of a disability, a health condition or a recommendation from your workstation assessment (see 9.4), such as a vertical mouse, a sit-stand desk, screen magnification software or a larger monitor, ask the service desk. Adjustments are paid from a central budget, not your team's budget, and are not limited by the replacement cycles in 8.1. If you join from another employer that provided specialist equipment through Access to Work, we will help you transfer or replace it.

### 8.4 Accounts, passwords and multi-factor authentication

You are responsible for the security of your accounts. The basic rules are:

- Use the company password manager for every work password. It generates long, unique passwords and fills them in for you.
- Never reuse a work password for a personal account, or the other way round.
- Sign in with your hardware security key wherever it is supported, which includes email, source control, the cloud consoles and Peoplebase. Keep the second key somewhere safe at home.
- Never give your password, security key or one-time code to anyone, including the service desk. Our IT staff will never ask for them.
- Lock your screen whenever you step away from your laptop, even in the office.

If you lose a security key, report it to the service desk the same day; it will be revoked and replaced.

### 8.5 Device security

All company laptops and phones are managed centrally. Disk encryption, the firewall, automatic updates and endpoint protection are switched on and must not be turned off. Software that is not in the self-service catalogue needs approval from IT; engineers have a separate process for development tools, set out in the engineering handbook.

Laptops restart to install updates on the second Tuesday of each month, between 12:00 and 14:00 unless you postpone the restart, which you can do for up to three days. Devices that fall more than two weeks behind on updates lose access to client systems until they are brought up to date.

### 8.6 Working away from the office

When working outside the office:

- Use your home network, a company phone hotspot or a trusted network. On public Wi-Fi in cafés, trains and hotels, connect the VPN before doing anything else.
- Position your screen so that people nearby cannot read it, and use the privacy filter supplied with your laptop when travelling.
- Do not take printed client documents home. If you must print, use the office printers, which release documents only when you tap your pass.
- Do not leave your laptop unattended in a car, even in the boot, or in a hotel room outside the safe.

### 8.7 Personal devices

You may read work email and chat on your own phone if you install the company management profile, which keeps work apps and data in a separate, encrypted container. IT can wipe that container remotely if the phone is lost or when you leave, but cannot see or erase your personal photos, messages or apps. Personal laptops and tablets must not be used for Harbourline work or to access client data.

### 8.8 Handling data

Everything we hold is classified as Public, Internal, Confidential or Restricted. Client data, including the names and addresses of tenants and residents in our clients' systems, is always Restricted. Restricted data must stay inside approved systems: it must never be copied to a personal device, emailed outside Harbourline, pasted into an external AI tool or uploaded to a file transfer service that is not on the approved list.

If you think data has been lost, sent to the wrong person or accessed by someone who should not have it, report it to security@harbourline-software.example or call the service desk immediately, day or night. We have 72 hours to decide whether to notify the regulator, so speed matters far more than blame. Nobody will be disciplined for reporting an honest mistake promptly.

### 8.9 Phishing and suspicious messages

Phishing emails and messages are the most common way attacks begin. Be wary of anything that asks you to sign in, pay an invoice, change bank details or open an unexpected attachment, especially if it is urgent. Use the "Report phishing" button in your mail client to send suspicious messages to the security team; do not forward them to colleagues. We run simulated phishing exercises four times a year, and the results are used only to plan training.

### 8.10 Acceptable use

Company systems are for work, but reasonable personal use, such as checking the news at lunch or ordering a delivery, is fine. You must not use company devices or networks to access illegal, offensive or sexually explicit material, to run a personal business, or to mine cryptocurrency. Activity on company systems is logged for security purposes. Logs are reviewed only where there is a specific security concern or a formal investigation, and any review of an individual's activity must be approved by the People Director and the Head of IT together.

### 8.11 Returning equipment

When your equipment is replaced or when you leave, return it to the service desk in person or with the prepaid courier box we send you. Please do not wipe devices yourself; the service desk does this securely and records it. See 12.7 for what happens when you leave.

## 9 Health, safety and wellbeing

### 9.1 Responsibilities

Harbourline is responsible for providing a safe and healthy workplace, whether that is Harbourline House, the Leeds office or your home office. Our Health and Safety Lead, Gareth Mullins, is the competent person who advises the board, and in Leeds the site lead, Priya Ramdhanie, carries the day-to-day responsibilities for that office. Every employee has a duty to take reasonable care of themselves and others, to follow safety instructions and to report hazards.

The health and safety policy statement, signed by the Chief Executive, is displayed in both offices and on the intranet. The Health and Safety Committee meets every quarter and includes elected employee representatives from both offices.

### 9.2 Fire safety

Each office has trained fire marshals, whose names and photographs are on the noticeboard by the kitchen. When the alarm sounds, leave by the nearest exit, do not stop to collect belongings and go to the assembly point: in Bristol, the open space on Gas Ferry Road opposite the main entrance; in Leeds, the corner of Wellington Row and Queen Street. Report to your fire marshal so that nobody is left unaccounted for.

The fire alarm in Bristol is tested every Wednesday at 10:00, and in Leeds every Thursday at 11:00. A short ring during the test does not mean you need to leave. Full evacuation drills take place twice a year without notice.

If you have a condition that would affect your ability to leave the building quickly, including a temporary one such as a broken leg or late pregnancy, the People team will agree a personal emergency evacuation plan with you.

### 9.3 First aid and accidents

There are at least four trained first aiders in Bristol and two in Leeds, and their names are listed on the intranet and next to each first aid kit. Defibrillators are kept at reception in both offices.

Report every accident, injury or near miss, however minor, using the online accident form, which goes to the Health and Safety Lead. Injuries that lead to more than seven days' absence must be reported to the Health and Safety Executive, so it is important that the form is completed promptly.

### 9.4 Workstations and display screen equipment

Most of us spend the working day at a screen, so a well set-up workstation matters. Everyone completes an online workstation assessment in their first week and again after a move or a change in health. The assessment covers both your office desk and your home setup. If it flags a problem, the service desk or the Health and Safety Lead will follow up within ten working days, and may arrange a visit or video call to check your setup.

Take regular breaks from the screen: a few minutes every hour is better than one long break. Eye tests are covered under 4.7.

### 9.5 Working at home safely

Your home workspace should have adequate lighting, a stable desk, a supportive chair and no trailing cables. We provide the chair and equipment described in 8.1 and the one-off allowance in 4.10. Check with your home insurer that working from home does not affect your policy; most insurers do not charge extra. If you have an accident while working at home, report it in the same way as an accident in the office.

### 9.6 Carrying heavy items and lone working

Office work rarely involves heavy loads, but deliveries, event kit and demonstration equipment can be awkward. Do not carry anything you find heavy or bulky on your own; ask a colleague or use the trolleys kept in the post room. Employees who visit client sites, especially empty properties or plant rooms, must follow the lone working procedure: tell a colleague where you are going, check in when you arrive and leave, and use the lone worker app on your company phone.

### 9.7 Mental health and wellbeing

We take mental health as seriously as physical health. Support available includes:

- **Mental health first aiders.** Twelve trained colleagues across both offices who will listen in confidence and help you find further support. Their names are on the intranet.
- **Employee Assistance Programme.** Up to eight sessions of counselling per issue, by phone, video or in person, and legal and financial information lines, all confidential and free to use (see 4.10).
- **Private medical cover.** Mental health treatment is included in the scheme described in 4.6.
- **Manager training.** Every manager completes a half-day course on supporting mental health at work within their first six months in the role.

If you are struggling, you do not have to tell your manager the details, but please tell someone. If you are worried about a colleague, ask how they are and point them towards these services.

### 9.8 Menopause, fertility and other health support

Colleagues experiencing menopause symptoms can ask for adjustments such as a desk near a window, a desk fan, flexible start times or more home working. Our menopause network meets monthly and is open to anyone affected, including partners. Time off for fertility treatment is covered in 7.5. We also run a monthly support group for parents and carers of disabled children, organised by colleagues and supported by the People team.

### 9.9 Alcohol, drugs and smoking

You must not work under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs. If you take prescribed medication that may affect your ability to work safely or drive, tell your manager. Alcohol is sometimes served at company events (see 10.6), and non-alcoholic drinks are always available. Smoking and vaping are not allowed anywhere inside our buildings or within five metres of the entrances.

## 10 Conduct, equality and grievances

### 10.1 How we expect people to behave

Our values are short: be straightforward, be kind, and finish what you start. In practice that means telling colleagues and clients the truth about progress and problems, treating everyone with respect even in a disagreement, and following through on commitments or saying early when you cannot. Most of this section describes what happens when things go wrong, but we hope you will rarely need it.

### 10.2 Equality, diversity and inclusion

Harbourline is committed to equal opportunity in recruitment, pay, promotion, training and every other part of working life. We do not tolerate discrimination on the grounds of any protected characteristic,[^7] and we go further than the law by also treating socio-economic background, caring responsibilities and accent as factors that must not affect how people are treated.

Some of the practical steps we take:

- Every job advert states the salary band, and interview panels include at least two people with mixed backgrounds and roles.
- Interview questions are agreed in advance and scored against the same criteria for every candidate.
- We publish our gender pay gap every April, although at our size we are not yet legally required to, and an ethnicity pay gap alongside it.
- Employee networks for women in technology, LGBTQ+ colleagues, disabled colleagues and neurodivergent colleagues, and for parents and carers, each have a small annual budget and an executive sponsor.

If you need a reasonable adjustment at any stage, from interview to leaving, ask your manager or the People team. You do not have to disclose a diagnosis to receive support.

### 10.3 Bullying and harassment

Bullying and harassment, including sexual harassment, are not acceptable at Harbourline, whether in the office, online, at client sites, at social events or on business trips. Harassment is unwanted conduct related to a protected characteristic that violates someone's dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment. Bullying is offensive, intimidating, malicious or insulting behaviour, or an abuse of power, that undermines or humiliates someone.

We take active steps to prevent sexual harassment, including annual training for everyone, a risk assessment for each company event, and a named contact for each event who is not drinking. Harassment by clients or suppliers is also covered: if a client contact behaves inappropriately towards you, tell your manager, and we will raise it with the client and change who works on the account if needed.

If you experience or witness bullying or harassment, you can raise it informally with the person concerned, if you feel able to, or report it to your manager, any People Partner, a mental health first aider or the confidential reporting line (see 10.8). All reports are taken seriously and investigated promptly.

### 10.4 Gifts, hospitality and conflicts of interest

You may accept small gifts and modest hospitality from clients and suppliers, such as a box of chocolates at Christmas or a working lunch, up to a value of £50. Anything above £50, or any gift of cash or vouchers, must be declined or declared in the gifts register on the intranet, and your manager will decide whether it can be kept. Never accept anything offered while a contract or procurement is being decided, and never offer anything to a public sector client contact without checking their organisation's rules first.

A conflict of interest arises where your personal interests could affect, or appear to affect, your decisions at work. Common examples include a close relative working for a supplier you choose, a second job with a competitor, or a role on the board of a client. Declare any potential conflict in Peoplebase as soon as you become aware of it. Most conflicts can be managed simply by making sure someone else takes the relevant decisions.

### 10.5 Outside work and social media

You may take on other paid work, voluntary roles or a small business of your own, provided it does not compete with Harbourline, involve our clients, use company time or equipment, or take your total working time above the limits in 3.7. Let your manager know before you start.

On social media, you are welcome to talk about your work at Harbourline in general terms and to repost our public announcements. Do not discuss clients, unreleased products, security incidents or colleagues' personal information, and make clear that opinions are your own. Only the Marketing team may post on behalf of the company.

### 10.6 Company events and alcohol

Company events are part of what makes Harbourline a good place to work, and they are also work. The standards in this section apply at the summer party, the winter dinner, team socials and conference evenings. Events that include alcohol always offer good non-alcoholic choices and food, and do not centre on drinking. Nobody should feel pressure to drink or to stay late. Taxis home from late company events are paid for by the company.

### 10.7 Disciplinary procedure

Where there are concerns about conduct, we first try to resolve them informally through a conversation with your manager. If that is not appropriate, or does not work, the formal disciplinary procedure applies. It follows the Acas Code of Practice and has these stages:

1. **Investigation.** A manager not involved in the matter gathers the facts and talks to you and any witnesses. In serious cases you may be suspended on full pay while this happens; suspension is not a disciplinary penalty.
2. **Hearing.** You receive the evidence in writing at least five working days before a hearing, and you may be accompanied by a colleague or trade union representative.
3. **Outcome.** Possible outcomes are no further action, a first written warning (on record for six months), a final written warning (on record for twelve months), or dismissal. Gross misconduct, such as theft, fraud, serious breaches of data security or violence, may lead to dismissal without notice.
4. **Appeal.** You can appeal in writing within ten working days. An appeal is heard by a more senior manager who has not been involved before, and their decision is final.

Concerns about performance, rather than conduct, are handled under the separate capability procedure, which focuses on support and clear goals before any formal warnings.

### 10.8 Raising a concern: whistleblowing

If you believe that something wrong is happening at Harbourline, such as fraud, a danger to health and safety, a breach of law or a cover-up, you should raise it. You can speak to your manager, any director, or the Chair of the Audit Committee, Dr Helen Marchbank, who is an independent non-executive director. You can also use the confidential reporting line run by an external provider, Clearline, by phone on 0800 046 0712 or online, anonymously if you prefer.

Employees who raise genuine concerns are protected by law and by this policy. Anyone who victimises a whistleblower will face disciplinary action. Personal grievances about your own treatment should normally be raised under the grievance procedure in 10.9 instead.

### 10.9 Grievance procedure and the Employee Forum

If you have a problem at work, try first to sort it out informally with your manager, or with a People Partner if the problem involves your manager. Many issues can be settled in a single conversation.

If informal discussion does not work, put your grievance in writing to the People team. We will:

- acknowledge it within three working days;
- arrange a meeting with an impartial manager, normally within ten working days, at which you may be accompanied by a colleague or trade union representative;
- investigate further if needed, and give you a written decision with reasons;
- offer an appeal to a director, which you must request within ten working days of the decision.

Collective issues can also be raised through the Employee Forum, a group of eight elected representatives from across both offices who meet the leadership team every two months. The Forum is consulted on changes to policies in this handbook (see 1.3) and publishes notes of every meeting on the intranet.

## 11 Learning and career development

### 11.1 Our approach

We hire people who like to learn, and we want you to keep learning while you are here. Most learning happens on the job: pairing, code reviews, design critiques, client workshops and stretch projects. Courses and conferences add to that, and so does time to read and experiment. Your manager's job includes helping you find the right mix.

### 11.2 Your personal learning budget

Every employee has a learning budget of £1,500 per calendar year, which can be spent on courses, conferences, books or certification exams. Part-time employees receive the full amount, not a pro rata amount, because the cost of a course does not depend on your hours. New starters receive a budget in proportion to the months remaining in the year, with a minimum of £500.

You decide how to use your budget, within these rules:

- The learning must relate to your current role or a role you could reasonably move into at Harbourline. This is interpreted generously: a front-end developer learning about accessibility law or a support analyst learning SQL both qualify.
- Book through the learning portal, which sends the request to your manager. Managers approve requests within five working days and should only refuse where the learning is clearly unrelated to work or the timing clashes with a firm commitment.
- Unspent budget does not carry over into the next year.
- Subscriptions to online learning platforms count, but the company already holds licences for two platforms, so check those first.

Conference tickets come from your learning budget, but getting there does not. Train fares, flights, hotels and meals for an approved conference are booked and claimed under section 5 and charged to your department's travel budget, so a conference in another city costs your learning budget only the price of the ticket.

### 11.3 Team and leadership development

Separately from the individual budgets, every manager can draw on £3,000 a year for training their team as a group, for example a two-day workshop on product discovery or a session with an external accessibility auditor. This team allowance cannot be used to top up any one person's individual budget.

New managers complete our six-month management programme, which covers coaching conversations, giving feedback, running fair recruitment, managing absence and wellbeing, and the basics of employment law. Experienced managers attend a leadership day twice a year.

### 11.4 Career framework and levels

Our career framework describes seven levels, from L1 (graduate and apprentice) to L7 (principal and director), with separate tracks for individual contributors and managers from L4 upwards. Each level sets out expectations for scope, impact, technical or functional skill, and how you work with others. You do not need to become a manager to progress: a principal engineer at L7 sits at the same level and salary band as a director.

The framework is published on the intranet, together with examples of work at each level from different teams.

### 11.5 Development conversations

Every employee has a development conversation with their manager twice a year, in February and August. It is a two-way conversation, not a rating exercise. You will discuss what has gone well, what you want to learn next, how you are progressing against the framework, and any support you need. The notes are written up in Peoplebase and you can add your own comments.

Between those conversations, you and your manager should meet one-to-one at least every two weeks.

### 11.6 Promotions

Promotions are decided in two rounds each year, in March and September. You can be put forward by your manager or nominate yourself. Each case is a short written statement with examples, reviewed by a panel from outside your team, including at least one person from another department. Panels look for evidence that you are already working consistently at the next level, not for time served.

Outcomes are given to each candidate within two weeks of the panel, with written feedback in every case. If you are not promoted, the feedback should make clear what is missing and how you might show it before the next round.

### 11.7 Study leave and qualifications

For formal qualifications such as a degree, a professional certificate or an apprenticeship, you can take up to five paid days of study leave a year for exams and exam preparation, on top of annual leave. Harbourline can also pay course fees above your personal learning budget for qualifications that are agreed as part of your development plan.

Where Harbourline pays more than £1,000 for a single qualification outside your personal budget, you agree to repay part of the cost if you resign within a year of completing it: 100% within six months, and 50% between six and twelve months. This does not apply to your personal learning budget, to training required for your role, or if you leave because of redundancy.

### 11.8 Mentoring, internal moves and apprenticeships

Anyone can ask for a mentor from another part of the company through the mentoring scheme, which matches people twice a year. Many experienced staff act as mentors, and mentoring time counts as work.

Internal vacancies are advertised on the intranet for at least one week before they are opened to outside candidates. You can apply after twelve months in your current role, or sooner with your manager's agreement. You do not need your manager's permission to apply, but we encourage you to tell them.

We run an apprenticeship programme with Bristol and Leeds colleges, taking on between six and ten apprentices a year in software development, data and IT support. Apprentices have a dedicated mentor and spend one day a week on their studies.

## 12 Leaving Harbourline

### 12.1 Before you decide

If you are thinking of leaving because something at work is not right, please talk to your manager or a People Partner first. A change of team, hours or role sometimes solves the problem, and we would rather know.

### 12.2 Resigning

To resign, write to your manager and copy the People team; an email is enough. Your manager will confirm your last working day in writing within three working days.

After probation, either party may end the employment with two months' written notice, effective at the end of a calendar month. So a resignation handed in on 10 June takes effect on 31 August. While you are still in probation, the much shorter period described in 2.5 applies instead, and the notice can end on any day of the week.

We may agree to release you earlier if your work can be handed over, but we are not obliged to.

### 12.3 Notice from Harbourline after long service

When Harbourline gives notice to someone with five or more years of continuous service, you receive whichever is longer: the contractual period in 12.2, or the statutory minimum for your length of service.[^8] For example, an employee with eleven years' service is entitled to eleven weeks, which is longer than two months.

### 12.4 Gardening leave and pay in lieu of notice

During notice, we may ask you to stay away from work and not contact clients or colleagues about business. This is called gardening leave. You remain employed, receive full pay and benefits, and must remain available to answer questions about handover. Gardening leave is most often used where someone is joining a competitor or has access to sensitive client information.

Alternatively, we may end your employment straight away and pay you a sum equal to the basic salary you would have earned during the notice period, known as payment in lieu of notice. This payment is taxed through payroll in the normal way.

### 12.5 Handover

In your last weeks, agree a handover plan with your manager. It should list your open work, your client contacts, any documents only you know where to find, and the systems you administer. Engineers should transfer ownership of repositories, cloud resources and on-call duties.

### 12.6 Final pay

Your final salary is paid on the normal pay day after your last day. It includes any holiday you have accrued but not taken, and Harbourline deducts any holiday taken beyond your accrued entitlement, along with outstanding loans such as the season ticket loan (see 4.9). Your P45 is sent by email within two weeks of your final pay.

### 12.7 Returning company property

Return your laptop, phone, security keys, building pass, company card and any other equipment by your last day, at the office or with the prepaid courier box. Do not keep copies of company or client data. Your accounts are closed at 18:00 on your last working day, and your email is redirected to your manager for 30 days.

### 12.8 Exit interviews and references

A People Partner will offer you an exit interview in your last fortnight. It is voluntary, and the themes, not individual comments, are reported to the leadership team every quarter.

Reference requests for former employees must be passed to the People team, who respond within five working days in the format described in 2.3.

### 12.9 Redundancy and retirement

If we ever need to reduce roles, we will consult fully, look for alternative roles and give at least the statutory notice. Harbourline pays redundancy pay at one and a half times the statutory amount, calculated on actual weekly pay rather than the statutory cap.

We have no fixed retirement age. If you are planning to retire, giving your manager an indication well ahead helps with succession planning, but you are not required to.
