## 1 Minutes of the last meeting

The minutes of the board meeting held on 17 January 2026 were approved as a true record, proposed by David Okafor and seconded by Gwen Llewellyn, with one correction: the date of the annual general meeting in item 6 should read 20 June 2026, not 13 June.

The Treasurer recommended that the board should keep the reserve at that level until the Brook Street inverters have been fully replaced. ## 3 Operations

Fiona Barrett reported that the five operating arrays generated 211 MWh in the eleven months, 4 percent above forecast. The replacement inverter at Brook Street was installed on 9 December; the remaining two units of the same age are showing reduced efficiency and should be replaced before summer. A quote of £5,600 for both has been received from Severnside Solar Services.

Decision: the board approved the replacement of the two remaining Brook Street inverters at a cost of up to £6,000, to be completed by the end of May.

## 4 Projects

### 4.1 Community hall rooftop array

Tom Whitfield reported that the planning consent and the lease with the Community Hall trustees are now signed. The 42 kWp array on the south roof of the hall will cost £38,500 including scaffolding, and will supply the hall during the day, with the surplus exported. The hall trustees will buy the electricity at 18p per kWh for twenty years.

Decision: the board gave final approval for the community hall array, with installation to begin in June 2026 once the hall's spring events programme has finished. Tom Whitfield will place the order with Severnside Solar Services.

### 4.2 Millbrook Primary School

Tom Whitfield reported that the school's business manager had raised concerns about the condition of the flat roof over the main teaching block. The installer's initial visit found signs of water staining in two classrooms, and the county council's property team has not yet confirmed the roof's remaining design life. Martin Sloane added that the governors would welcome the project but could not accept any risk of roof damage during term time.

The board discussed the risk of committing to a supply contract before the roof's condition is known. The board agreed to defer the photovoltaic installation at Millbrook Primary until the structural survey of the roof is complete. The county council has commissioned the survey from Hartley Stone Engineers and expects the report by the end of April. If the survey finds that the roof needs repair, the board will ask the council whether the repair can be brought forward so that the panels can be fitted in the summer holidays of 2027 instead.

Martin Sloane did not vote on this item.

### 4.3 Wind turbine share offer

Gwen Llewellyn and Tom Whitfield presented an outline for a new community share offer to fund a single 250 kW wind turbine on land at Higher Combe Farm, subject to planning. The landowner has signed heads of terms for a 25-year lease. The total project cost is estimated at £640,000. The proposal is to raise £400,000 from members and the public through withdrawable shares, with the rest from a community energy loan fund.

Points raised in discussion:

- The minimum investment should stay at £100 so that local households on low incomes can take part.
- The offer document must give a clear and fair account of the risks, including the risk that planning permission is refused.
- An independent review of the financial model is needed before the offer opens.
- The offer should not open until the result of the planning application is known, expected in September 2026.

Decision: the board agreed in principle to prepare a share offer for the wind turbine, and asked Gwen Llewellyn and David Okafor to bring a draft offer timetable and budget to the May meeting. No money will be spent beyond £2,500 for the independent review until then.

### 4.4 Battery storage feasibility study

The board considered three quotes for a feasibility study into adding battery storage to the Ferry Road and community hall sites. The study would look at whether storage can increase the share of generation used on site and earn income from flexibility services.

Decision: the board commissioned the study from Westcountry Energy Advice at a fixed price of £4,200, funded half from the Riverside Parish Council energy grant. The report is due by 31 July 2026.

## 5 Membership

Gwen Llewellyn reported that the co-operative has 412 members, an increase of 19 since January. Most new members joined after the open day at the community hall on 21 February. Five members have asked to withdraw shares totalling £3,750; the board approved the withdrawals, which are within the limit set in the rules for this financial year.

The newsletter will move to four issues a year, with the next issue in April covering the community hall array and the plans for the wind turbine. Gwen Llewellyn asked directors to send short items by 3 April.

Decisions:

- The five share withdrawals were approved.
- The board agreed to hold a members' information evening on the wind turbine proposal on 23 April 2026.
