# Eastbrook Public Library Service — Annual Report 2025

Eastbrook Borough Council, Culture and Communities Directorate. Published March 2026.

## 1 Foreword by the Head of Service

2025 was a year of change for Eastbrook's libraries. We opened our doors on a Sunday for the first time in fourteen years, we moved the whole of our digital lending to a new platform, and we said goodbye to one of our longest-serving colleagues, Margaret Huxley, who retired from Westmoor Library after 38 years. At the same time, the number of physical books borrowed continued its slow decline, and our budget is still lower in real terms than it was a decade ago.

None of this is unusual for a public library service in England. What I hope this report shows is how Eastbrook's libraries have responded: by listening to residents about when they want to visit, by putting money where demand is growing, and by working with partners who reach people we cannot reach on our own. More than 926,000 visits were made to our eight branches last year. Behind each visit is someone looking for a quiet place to work, a story for a child, help with a form, or simply some company on a winter afternoon.

I want to thank the 64 members of staff and 212 volunteers who made all of this happen, and the Friends of Eastbrook Library, whose fundraising has made Sunday opening possible. I also thank the councillors on the Communities Scrutiny Committee for their careful challenge throughout the year.

Rebecca Thornley, Head of Library and Information Services

## 2 The year in numbers

### 2.1 Headlines

- 926,700 visits to our eight branches, up 2.1 percent on 2024.
- 38,940 active borrowers, meaning members who borrowed at least one item during the year.
- 4,120 new members joined, 1,380 of them children under 12.
- 61,400 hours of public computer use.
- 2,870 events and learning sessions, attended by 54,300 people.

Total loans fell by 3.2 percent to 412,580, while e-book loans rose by 27 percent. The fall is entirely in physical items: loans from branches dropped by 7.7 percent, while everything borrowed through the online service went up by almost a fifth. Visits rose even though physical loans fell, which matches what we see every day: more people come in to study, to use the computers and Wi-Fi, or to attend an event, and they do not always leave with a book.

Children's books remain the most resilient part of our physical stock, thanks to the Summer Reading Challenge and our weekly rhyme times. Audiobooks, counting the CD and downloadable versions together, went up by 11 percent, even though CD loans fell sharply; listeners have moved to their phones. We will stop buying new DVDs from April 2026 and will sell off the remaining DVD stock at the Central Library book sale in the autumn.[^1]

### 2.4 Membership and reach

At the end of 2025, 38,940 people had borrowed at least once in the year, which is 21 percent of Eastbrook's population. Children's membership grew for the third year in a row, mainly because every reception class in the borough now visits its local library in the first term. The share of active borrowers living in the borough's most deprived neighbourhoods rose from 17 to 19 percent, which we attribute to the removal of overdue fines for adults in 2024.

### 2.5 What users told us

In October we ran our two-yearly user survey in all branches and online, and received 2,214 replies. Overall, 91 percent of users rated the service as good or very good, the same as in 2023. Staff helpfulness again received the highest score, with 96 percent satisfied. The lowest scores were for the range of new adult non-fiction (68 percent satisfied) and for the number of computers available at busy times at Central Library (64 percent).

When asked what would make them use the library more, the most common answers were:

- longer opening hours, especially in the early evening and at weekends (41 percent);
- more copies of popular new books, to shorten waiting lists (29 percent);
- more comfortable places to sit and study (22 percent);
- more activities for teenagers after school (14 percent).

We have used these results to set several of our priorities for 2026 (see section 9). A full summary of the survey is available at every branch on request.

## 3 Opening hours and access

### 3.1 Sunday opening at Central Library

For years, residents have told us in surveys that weekends are the only time they can visit. In 2024, the Friends of Eastbrook Library offered to fund a trial of Sunday opening from their own fundraising and from a legacy left by a long-standing member, Mr Harold Pennington. From 6 April, the Central Library opened on Sundays from 12:00 to 17:00, funded by the Friends of Eastbrook Library.

Between April and December the Central Library was open on 39 Sundays and received 21,860 visits, an average of 560 a Sunday. Sunday visitors were younger than our weekday users: 38 percent were under 35, compared with 22 percent on weekdays. The study area on the second floor was the busiest space, and the children's library hosted a monthly family storytelling session. The Friends have agreed to fund the trial for a second year, and the council will decide in the 2027 budget whether to fund it permanently.

All other branches remain closed on Sundays. We looked at opening a second branch on Sundays but could not find the staffing within the current budget.

### 3.2 Northgate Saturday hours

Since 6 September, Northgate Library has stayed open until 16:00 on Saturdays, three hours later than before. The change was paid for by moving three hours from Wednesday afternoon, when the branch was at its quietest. Saturday visits at Northgate rose by 34 percent in the last four months of the year compared with the same months of 2024.

### 3.4 Physical access

Every branch now has step-free access from the street. The last branch without it, Hollins Park, gained a new ramped entrance and automatic doors in July, funded by a £48,000 grant from the council's capital accessibility programme. Changing Places toilets are available at Central and Southfield. Large-print and audio stock is held at every branch, and the home library service (see 6.3) reaches people who cannot come in at all.

During the year we also trained 41 staff as dementia-friendly champions, and Central, Northgate and Westmoor now offer a quiet hour every Tuesday morning, with lights dimmed and no announcements, for visitors who find busy spaces difficult.

## 4 Events and learning

### 4.1 Children and families

Rhyme time and baby bounce sessions ran every week in all eight branches during term time, with 1,120 sessions and 23,400 attendances by children and their carers. The Summer Reading Challenge, themed "Story Garden" this year, signed up 4,860 children, of whom 2,910 read all six books and received a medal. Southfield had the highest completion rate at 71 percent, after the branch team visited every primary school in its area during the summer term.

Class visits continued to grow. 214 classes from 46 schools visited a library during the year, and every child in reception received a library card in a welcome pack. The Lego club at Ashby Green, run by two volunteers, has a waiting list of 30 children.

### 4.2 Adult learning

We ran 410 adult learning sessions in partnership with Eastbrook Adult Community College. The most popular were:

- English conversation groups for speakers of other languages, at Central and Northgate, with 3,850 attendances;
- "Get online" sessions for beginners, covering email, video calls and online forms (see 5.3);
- family history workshops using our local studies collection, which were fully booked every month;
- a six-week course on managing household bills, run with the Eastbrook Citizens Advice service.

### 4.3 Reading groups and author events

Fifty-two reading groups meet in our libraries or borrow sets of books through the reading group service, which holds 640 titles in sets of ten copies. Highlights of the author programme included a sold-out evening with crime writer Elaine Faraday at Central Library in October, and a poetry workshop for teenagers led by Kofi Mensah-Hart, Eastbrook's young poet laureate for 2025.

### 4.4 Local studies

The local studies and archives room at Central Library answered 1,940 enquiries. Volunteers finished cataloguing the Wainwright photographic collection, 3,200 glass plate negatives of Eastbrook street scenes taken between 1890 and 1925. Scans of the first 800 plates can now be viewed at [eastbrook-libraries.example.org/heritage](https://eastbrook-libraries.example.org/heritage).

### 4.5 Young people

Teenagers are the group we find hardest to keep. Library use drops sharply between the ages of 12 and 16, and our survey showed that many teenagers see the library as a place for younger children. In 2025 we tried three things to change this. Central and Northgate started a weekly after-school gaming and board games club, which drew an average of 22 young people a session. A teen reading panel of 15 members chose £6,000 of new young adult stock for all branches. And during the summer, eight young volunteers aged 14 to 17 helped run the Summer Reading Challenge, earning hours towards their Duke of Edinburgh's Award.

## 5 Digital services

### 5.1 The new digital lending platform

In June we moved e-books, e-audiobooks and e-magazines to a single app, replacing three separate services that each needed its own login. Members now sign in once with their library card number and PIN. The move took longer than planned, and the app was unavailable for four days in the middle of June while records were transferred. We apologise to the members affected and have extended all loans that were due during that period.

The new platform has made it easier to buy titles that are in demand. We now buy extra copies of any e-book with more than five people waiting for it, and the average wait for a popular title fell from 41 days in January to 19 days in December. Spending on e-books and e-audiobooks rose to £148,000, from £112,000 in 2024.[^2]

### 5.2 Public computers and Wi-Fi

All 142 public computers were replaced in the spring with new machines that start faster and support screen readers and magnifiers. The first hour each day is free for members; after that, sessions cost £1 an hour, except for job seekers, who use the computers free of charge on proof of benefit. Wi-Fi is free in every branch and was used for 214,000 sessions, a rise of 12 percent.

Printing and scanning are now available from personal phones and laptops at Central, Northgate and Southfield. Black and white printing costs 15p a page, and colour 50p.

### 5.3 Digital skills support

The "Get online" programme supported 1,680 people, most of them over 65, to use email, book doctors' appointments online and apply for a parking permit or a council tax discount. Twelve "digital buddies", volunteers trained by our partner Connect Eastbrook, offered one-to-one help in every branch at fixed times each week. A device loan scheme, funded by the Eastbrook Community Foundation, lent 85 tablets with free data to residents who had no internet at home, for up to three months at a time.

### 5.4 Online catalogue and enquiries

The online catalogue received 1.12 million searches, and members placed 58,300 reservations, 71 percent of them online. Reservations on physical books are free for children and cost 50p for adults. The "Ask a librarian" service answered 3,460 enquiries by email and online chat, with a median reply time of five working hours. The most common topics were account and PIN problems, family history, and help finding reliable health information.

## 6 Community partnerships

### 6.1 Health and wellbeing

The Books on Prescription scheme, run with Eastbrook's GP practices, offers recommended self-help books on topics such as anxiety, sleep problems and long-term pain. GPs and mental health workers issued 1,260 recommendations, and the books were borrowed 3,740 times. The health information point at Central Library hosted weekly drop-ins by the NHS stop-smoking service and monthly blood pressure checks by the community pharmacy team.

### 6.2 Warm spaces

From November to March, all eight branches were registered as warm spaces, with free hot drinks, newspapers and a friendly welcome. Staff counted 9,120 visits by people who said they had come in mainly to keep warm. Eastbrook Food Bank left leaflets and vouchers at every branch, and the council's money advice team held drop-ins at Southfield and Kingsbridge.

### 6.3 Home library service

The home library service delivers books, audiobooks and large-print titles every four weeks to residents who cannot visit a branch because of illness, disability or caring responsibilities. In 2025, 318 people used the service, including 46 residents in seven care homes. Twenty-six volunteer drivers made 3,980 deliveries. The service van was replaced in August with a smaller model that can reach the narrow streets of the old town.

### 6.4 Schools, colleges and employers

We worked with Eastbrook Sixth Form College to offer study space at Central Library during the exam season, with extended access to the second-floor study area from 08:00 on weekdays in May and June. The Jobs and Skills hub at Northgate, run with the Department for Work and Pensions and two local employers, helped 412 people with CVs and job applications; 96 of them told us they had found work.

### 6.5 New arrivals in Eastbrook

Working with the Eastbrook Welcome Partnership, we offered library membership without proof of address to people seeking asylum and to refugees living in the borough's temporary accommodation. 236 people joined this way during the year. Central Library now holds a collection of 1,800 books in Arabic, Pashto, Polish, Somali, Tigrinya and Ukrainian, chosen with community advisers, and stock in these languages was borrowed 5,120 times. A weekly conversation café at Westmoor brings new arrivals together with local volunteers to practise English over tea.

## 7 Staff and volunteers

### 7.1 Staff

The service employed 64 members of staff at the end of the year, equal to 47.2 full-time posts. Eleven people joined and nine left. We introduced a new role of community librarian at Southfield and Kingsbridge, focused on outreach rather than counter work. Sickness absence fell to 7.8 days per employee, from 9.4 days in 2024.

All front-line staff completed training in customer care, safeguarding and the new digital lending platform. Six library assistants started a library and information apprenticeship at level 3, funded through the council's apprenticeship levy.

### 7.2 Volunteers

212 volunteers gave 18,640 hours of their time, the equivalent of about ten full-time staff. They help with the home library service, digital buddies, the Summer Reading Challenge, local studies, reading groups and Lego clubs. Volunteers do not replace paid staff; every branch has at least one member of staff on duty during opening hours.

We held a volunteers' celebration at Central Library in December, where the Mayor of Eastbrook presented long-service certificates to 14 volunteers with more than ten years of service.[^3]

### 7.3 The Friends of Eastbrook Library

The Friends have 280 members and raised £41,600 during the year from book sales, a quiz night, a sponsored walk along the river path and the Pennington legacy. Besides Sunday opening, they paid for new seating in the Central Library children's area and for prizes in the Summer Reading Challenge.

The service costs Eastbrook residents about £31.90 per head each year, compared with an average of £33.40 for similar councils.[^4]

## 9 Priorities for 2026

1. Decide, with the Friends and the council, how Sunday opening at Central Library can continue beyond the trial.
2. Review opening hours at Ashby Green and Elmhurst, the two branches with the shortest hours, using the results of the residents' survey due in May.
3. Increase spending on e-books and e-audiobooks by a further £25,000 and keep the average wait for popular titles below three weeks.
4. Extend the device loan scheme to 150 tablets and offer "Get online" sessions in all eight branches.
5. Start the refurbishment of the Central Library ground floor, including a new self-service area and a larger events space, subject to the council's capital programme.
6. Recruit 30 new volunteers, especially young people aged 16 to 24, through a partnership with Eastbrook Sixth Form College.
7. Publish quarterly performance figures on the library service's web pages so that residents can follow progress during the year.

The service will report progress against these priorities to the Communities Scrutiny Committee in October 2026, and again in next year's annual report, which will include the results of the residents' survey on opening hours.
