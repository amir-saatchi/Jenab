This manual covers the AeroTherm 9 monobloc outdoor unit (model AT9-M) and the matching AeroTherm hydraulic module with integrated controller (model AT9-HM). Read the whole manual before you install, commission or use the appliance, and keep it near the indoor unit for the life of the system. Sections 1 to 4 are written for the qualified installer. ## 1 Safety

### 1.1 Symbols used in this manual

- **Warning** marks a hazard that could cause death or serious injury if the instruction is ignored.
- **Caution** marks a hazard that could damage the appliance or the property.
- **Note** marks advice that helps the system run efficiently.

### 1.2 Who may work on the appliance

Installation, commissioning, repair and any work that opens the refrigerant circuit must be carried out by a person holding a valid certificate for handling flammable refrigerants. The AeroTherm 9 contains 1.15 kg of R290 (propane), which is a highly flammable gas of safety group A3.[^1] Electrical work must be carried out by a competent electrician and must comply with the national wiring regulations in force at the time of installation.

The householder may operate the controller, read the display, clean the outside of the casing, clear leaves from around the unit and clean the water filter as described in 7.3. The householder must not remove any panel that is fixed with screws.

### 1.3 General warnings

- **Warning:** Switch off the appliance at the local isolator and at the consumer unit before removing any panel. Capacitors in the inverter board can hold a dangerous charge for up to five minutes after the supply is cut.
- **Warning:** Do not use naked flames, smoke or operate spark-producing tools within 3 m of the outdoor unit while any panel is removed.
- **Warning:** If you smell gas near the outdoor unit, switch off the appliance at the isolator, keep people away and call the Calderwick helpline. Do not switch lights or other equipment on or off near the unit.
- **Caution:** Water in the flow pipe can reach 70 °C during the legionella cycle. Do not touch uninsulated pipework next to the hydraulic module.
- **Caution:** Do not climb on the outdoor unit or use it as a step or shelf.
- **Caution:** Children must not play with the appliance. Cleaning by the user must not be done by children without supervision.

## 2 Product overview and technical data

### 2.1 How the system works

The outdoor unit draws heat from the outside air through a finned coil, raises it to a useful temperature with a variable-speed compressor and passes it to the heating water through a plate heat exchanger. The whole refrigerant circuit is sealed inside the outdoor unit, so only water pipes run between the outdoor unit and the building. Inside, the hydraulic module holds the circulation pump, a 3-way diverter valve for hot water, a backup immersion heater, the expansion vessel, the pressure relief valve and the controller.

## 3 Installation

### 3.1 Before you start

Check the heat loss calculation for the building before you order the unit. The AeroTherm 9 is sized for properties with a design heat loss between 5.5 kW and 8.5 kW at the local design outdoor temperature. Check also that the existing radiators can deliver the design heat output at a flow temperature of 50 °C or below; replace undersized radiators where needed.

Handle the outdoor unit upright at all times. If it has to be tipped for access, do not exceed 30° from the vertical, and leave it upright for at least one hour before you switch it on, so that the compressor oil can settle back.

### 3.2 Siting the outdoor unit

Choose a position that meets all of the following:

- The base is a level concrete slab or a pair of concrete plinths that can carry at least 250 kg, raised at least 150 mm above the ground so that snow does not build up under the coil.
- Air can flow freely to the back of the unit and away from the fan at the front. Do not place the fan facing a wall closer than 1.5 m, or the cold air will recirculate into the coil.
- The unit is not in a hollow, a sunken patio or a light well. Propane is heavier than air and could collect there in the unlikely event of a leak.
- There are no drains, cellar openings, air bricks or gullies within 1 m of the unit.
- Condensate and defrost water can drain away without forming ice on paths or steps.
- The position meets the local planning noise limits at the nearest neighbouring window.

### 3.3 Clearances

| Side | Minimum clearance | Recommended clearance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Back (air inlet) | 300 mm | 500 mm |
| Left side (service side) | 500 mm | 800 mm |
| Right side | 300 mm | 500 mm |
| Front (fan outlet) | 1,500 mm | 2,000 mm |
| Above | 1,000 mm | open sky |

Keep the protection zone around the unit free of ignition sources. The zone extends 1 m from the casing on all sides and down to the ground, and must not include any opening into the building such as a window that can be opened, a door, or a vent.

### 3.4 Hydraulic installation

Connect the outdoor unit to the hydraulic module with insulated pipe of at least 28 mm copper or 32 mm multilayer pipe. Use the flexible hoses supplied at the outdoor unit to stop vibration passing into the building. Insulate all outdoor pipework with closed-cell insulation at least 19 mm thick, protected against ultraviolet light.

Fit the strainer assembly supplied with the unit on the return pipe, as close to the hydraulic module as possible, with isolating valves either side so that it can be cleaned without draining the system. The strainer has a 500 micron mesh and a magnetic core. Do not leave it out: debris from old radiators is the most common cause of damage to the plate heat exchanger and of low-flow faults.

Fit the drain valve V3 at the lowest point of the outdoor pipe run, where it leaves the building. Valve V3 must be reachable without tools from inside or from a protected position outside, because the householder may need it in an emergency (see 6.3).

Flush the existing heating system with a cleaning chemical before you connect the new unit, then fill with clean water and add an inhibitor at the dose given by its maker. Record the inhibitor brand and the date on the commissioning checklist.

Where every radiator has a thermostatic valve, fit a buffer tank of at least 40 litres so that the minimum system volume is always open to the heat pump.

### 3.5 Electrical installation

The outdoor unit and the hydraulic module need separate supplies:

1. Outdoor unit: 230 V, 20 A type C circuit breaker with a type A residual current device of 30 mA, cable 4 mm² minimum, through a lockable rotary isolator within sight of the unit.
2. Hydraulic module and backup heater: 230 V, 16 A type C circuit breaker, cable 2.5 mm² minimum.
3. Control cable between the units: two-core screened cable, 0.75 mm², maximum length 50 m. Keep it at least 50 mm away from mains cables along its whole run.

Connect the outdoor air temperature sensor on the north or north-east wall of the building, at least 2 m above the ground, away from windows, flues and direct sun. A sensor on a sunny wall makes the controller believe it is warmer outside than it is.

### 3.6 Condensate

In heating mode the outdoor coil produces up to 25 litres of water a day in wet weather, and much more during defrost. Lead the condensate either to a soakaway filled with gravel at least 500 mm deep or to a drain through a trap. Fit the trace heater kit CCS-TH2 to any condensate pipe that runs above ground in places where the outdoor temperature regularly drops below freezing.

## 4 Commissioning

### 4.1 Checks before first start

Work through the commissioning checklist supplied in the box and sign each line. In particular:

1. Check that the system pressure is between 1.2 and 1.5 bar when cold, and that the expansion vessel pre-charge is set to 1.0 bar.
2. Open all radiator valves and all isolating valves, including both valves either side of the strainer.
3. Vent the hydraulic module through the automatic air vent and vent every radiator. Air in the plate heat exchanger causes noise and low-flow faults.
4. Check that the outdoor unit has been standing upright for at least one hour.
5. Check that the condensate drain is clear and that the protection zone (see 3.3) is free of ignition sources.
6. Measure the supply voltage at the isolator. It must be between 207 V and 253 V.

### 4.2 First start

Switch on the hydraulic module first, then the outdoor unit. The controller starts the setup assistant in English; change the language under Menu > Settings > Language if needed. The assistant asks for:

- the date and time;
- the type of heat emitters (radiators, underfloor heating or a mix);
- the design flow temperature at the design outdoor temperature;
- whether a hot water cylinder is connected and its volume;
- whether the backup heater is allowed to run for space heating, for hot water, for both or only in an emergency.

From these answers the controller sets an initial heating curve. For radiators designed for 50 °C at −3 °C outside, the assistant chooses curve 0.8; for underfloor heating it chooses curve 0.4. You can refine the curve later (see 5.4).

### 4.3 Pump and flow setting

Run the pump test under Menu > Installer > Pump test. The controller runs the circulation pump at each speed step and shows the measured flow. Choose the lowest step that gives at least 22 l/min with every heating zone open. If you cannot reach 15 l/min, look for closed valves, a blocked strainer or air in the system before going further. Running the unit below the minimum flow rate will cause error E03 and can freeze the plate heat exchanger during defrost.

### 4.4 Handover

Before you leave the site:

- show the householder where the isolator, the strainer and valve V3 are, and go through section 6 together;
- register the product at [calderwick-climate.example.org/register](https://calderwick-climate.example.org/register) within 30 days, which is a condition of the extended warranty (see section 9);
- leave the signed commissioning checklist with this manual.

## 5 Operation and the controller

### 5.1 The display

The controller on the front of the hydraulic module has a 4.3-inch colour touch screen and a single rotary knob. The home screen shows:

- the outdoor temperature;
- the current flow temperature and its target;
- the hot water temperature in the cylinder;
- the active operating mode as an icon (sun for heating, tap for hot water, snowflake for defrost, moon for set-back);
- a bell symbol when there is a message or an error.

Press the knob once to open the main menu. Turn it to scroll, press to select, and hold for two seconds to go back to the home screen.

### 5.2 Operating modes

| Mode | What the unit does |
| --- | --- |
| Auto | Heats the house according to the heating curve and the time programme, and heats hot water at the programmed times. |
| Comfort | Holds the comfort room temperature all day, ignoring set-back periods. |
| Eco | Holds the set-back temperature all day; hot water at the eco temperature. |
| Hot water only | Summer mode. No space heating; frost protection stays active. |
| Holiday | Set-back temperature and no hot water until the return date; a legionella cycle runs on the day before return. |
| Standby | No heating, no hot water. The anti-freeze function stays active as long as the unit has power. |

### 5.3 Time programmes

You can set up to four heating periods and two hot water periods a day, separately for each day of the week. The factory programme for heating is 06:00 to 22:00 at 20 °C, with a set-back to 17 °C overnight. Keep set-backs small: a larger drop usually costs more to recover than it saves.

### 5.4 Adjusting the heating curve

The heating curve links the outdoor temperature with the flow temperature. A steeper curve gives hotter water on cold days. Change the curve in small steps:

- If the rooms are too cold in mild weather but fine in cold weather, raise the parallel shift by 1 K.
- If the rooms are fine in mild weather but too cold in cold weather, raise the slope by 0.1.
- Wait at least two days between changes, because the building responds slowly.

### 5.5 Hot water and the legionella cycle

The heat pump heats the cylinder to 48 °C by default. Once a week, on Sunday at 02:00, the controller raises the cylinder to 60 °C using the backup heater to control legionella bacteria. You can change the day and time under Menu > Hot water > Legionella, but the cycle cannot be switched off from the user menu.

### 5.6 Silent mode

Silent mode limits the compressor and fan speed to keep the sound level at least 4 dB(A) below normal. It is useful at night where the outdoor unit is close to a neighbour's bedroom. In silent mode the heat output at −7 °C falls to around 5.8 kW, so in a cold spell the backup heater may run more often. Set silent mode times under Menu > Settings > Silent mode.

## 6 Frost protection

### 6.1 The built-in anti-freeze function

The AeroTherm 9 protects itself against frost automatically while it is switched on. When the water temperature in the outdoor unit drops to around 4 °C, the controller starts the circulation pump to move warmer water from the building through the outdoor unit. If the water keeps falling and reaches about 2 °C, the compressor or the backup heater starts to warm the circuit. This function works in every operating mode, including standby, but it depends entirely on the unit having an electricity supply and on the water being able to circulate. Do not close the isolating valves on the heating circuit in winter, and do not switch the unit off at the isolator when you go away; use holiday mode instead (see 5.2).

### 6.2 Defrost

In cold, damp weather a layer of frost or ice forms on the fins of the outdoor coil. This is normal and does not mean the unit is faulty. Every 30 to 90 minutes, depending on the weather, the unit reverses its cycle for a few minutes to melt the ice. During defrost you may see a cloud of steam rising from the unit, the fan stops and water runs out underneath. The snowflake icon is shown on the display. Contact your installer only if the coil stays covered in a solid block of ice for more than a day, or if ice builds up underneath the unit and starts to reach the coil.

### 6.3 Power cuts in winter

A power cut stops the anti-freeze function, and the pipework outside the building is then at risk. Short power cuts are not a problem in most weather, because the water in the system holds enough heat. If the electricity supply fails for longer than two hours when the outdoor temperature is below 0 °C, drain the water circuit through valve V3 or fill it with the approved glycol mixture to protect it against frost damage. Valve V3 is the drain valve fitted at the lowest point of the outdoor pipework (see 3.4). Collect the water in a bucket or lead it away with a hose. Close V3 again and ask your installer to refill and vent the system once the supply is back.

Systems that are already filled with glycol do not need to be drained. The approved mixture is Calderwick ThermoGuard propylene glycol at 30 percent by volume, which protects down to about −15 °C. A glycol filling reduces the heat output by around 4 percent and must be checked every year with a refractometer (see the table in 7.1).[^3]
## 7 Maintenance

Regular maintenance keeps the heat pump efficient and is a condition of the warranty. Some tasks can be done by the householder; others need a qualified engineer.

### 7.1 Maintenance schedule

| Task | Interval | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Check the system pressure on the display (1.2 to 2.0 bar when warm) | Monthly | Householder |
| Clear leaves, snow and debris from around the outdoor unit | Monthly in autumn and winter | Householder |
| Check that the condensate drain runs freely | Every three months | Householder |
| Check the outside pipe insulation for damage from birds and sunlight | Every six months | Householder |
| Clean the outdoor coil with a soft brush and low-pressure water | Every 12 months | Engineer or householder |
| Check the refrigerant circuit for leaks with a gas detector | Every 12 months | Certified engineer |
| Check the expansion vessel pre-charge | Every 12 months | Engineer |
| Test the pressure relief valve by turning its cap | Every 12 months | Engineer |
| Test the inhibitor level and, where fitted, the glycol concentration | Every 12 months | Engineer |
| Check electrical connections and tighten terminals | Every 24 months | Electrician or engineer |
| Replace the anode in the hot water cylinder (enamelled cylinders only) | Every 36 months | Engineer |

### 7.2 Annual service

Once a year, ideally before the heating season starts in September or October, a Calderwick-approved engineer must service the appliance. The service includes a leak test of the refrigerant circuit, a check of the compressor current against the commissioning values, a check of the fan bearings and a download of the error log. The engineer records the service on the Calderwick service portal. Without these records the extended warranty does not apply.

### 7.3 Cleaning the water filter

The strainer on the return pipe catches magnetite, scale and other debris carried by the heating water. Clean the water filter (strainer) on the return pipe every six months, and after the first two weeks of operation. A blocked strainer lowers the flow rate, reduces efficiency and can trigger error E03. To clean it:

1. Switch the controller to standby and wait until the pump stops.
2. Close the isolating valves on both sides of the strainer.
3. Put a towel and a small bowl under the strainer, then unscrew the bottom cap by hand.
4. Pull out the magnetic core and the mesh basket. Wipe the black sludge off the magnet and rinse the mesh under the tap.
5. Check the O-ring for damage and replace it if it is cracked (spare part CCS-OR28).
6. Refit the basket, the magnet and the cap, and open both isolating valves slowly.
7. Check the system pressure. If it has dropped below 1.2 bar, top it up through the filling loop to 1.5 bar.
8. Switch the controller back to its normal mode.

If the mesh is still heavily blocked at the six-month cleaning, ask your installer to check the water quality and the inhibitor.

### 7.4 Cleaning the casing and the outdoor coil

Wipe the casing with a damp cloth. Never use solvents or a pressure washer; the coil fins bend easily. Brush dust and leaves off the coil gently in the direction of the fins, then rinse with a garden hose at normal mains pressure. Switch the unit off at the isolator before cleaning the coil, and keep water away from the electrical box.

## 8 Error codes

### 8.1 How errors are shown

When the controller detects a fault it shows a bell symbol on the home screen and a code on the message screen. Codes starting with E are faults: the unit stops the affected function until the fault is cleared. Codes starting with P are protection states: the unit limits or pauses its operation by itself and normally recovers without help once conditions return to normal. The last 50 events are stored under Menu > Info > Error log.

To reset an E code, correct the cause, then hold the knob for five seconds on the message screen. If the same E code appears three times within 24 hours, the controller locks the outdoor unit and only an engineer can release it.

### 8.3 Before you call service

Have ready the serial number from the type plate on the right-hand side of the outdoor unit, the error code and when it first appeared, and the name of the installer. Many first-winter faults turn out to be a blocked strainer, closed radiator valves or low system pressure, so check those first.[^4]

## 10 Disposal

At the end of its life the AeroTherm 9 must not be put in household waste. A certified engineer must recover the refrigerant before the outdoor unit is moved from site; venting R290 to the atmosphere is not permitted, even though its climate impact is low.[^5]

Take the hydraulic module and the controller to a collection point for waste electrical and electronic equipment, or ask your installer to take them back. Drain the heating water and any glycol mixture into a container and hand the glycol to the local waste site; do not pour it into a drain or onto the ground.
