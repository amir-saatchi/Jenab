# pair-10

**Task:**

I work full time and want to pass the German B1 exam in 6 months, starting from A2. I can study about 1 hour on weekdays and 3 hours on weekend days. How should I plan it?

---

## Answer A

Balancing a full-time job while preparing for the German B1 exam from an A2 level in 6 months is entirely achievable, provided you stick to a disciplined routine. Your schedule yields about **11 hours of study per week** (1 hour on weekdays, 3 hours on each weekend day), giving you a total of roughly **280 hours** over 26 weeks. 

Here is a structured, milestone-based self-study plan designed to maximize your time, cover all four exam modules (*Hören, Lesen, Schreiben, Sprechen*), and keep burnout at bay.

---

### The 26-Week Milestone Plan

Divide your 6 months into three distinct phases:

#### Phase 1: Grammar and Vocabulary Foundation (Weeks 1–8)
* **Objective:** Bridge the gap between A2 and B1 by solidifying foundational grammar (e.g., *Nebensätze*, passive voice, subjunctive/Konjunktiv II) and expanding your core vocabulary.
* **Weekday 60-Minute Routine:** 
  * 15 mins: Anki spaced repetition for vocabulary.
  * 30 mins: Grammar drills from an A2/B1 transition workbook.
  * 15 mins: Short reading or listening exercise (e.g., *Nachrichten Leicht*).
* **Weekend 3-Hour Blocks:** Dedicate these to deeper grammar reviews, tackling tricky sentence structures, and doing longer listening comprehension exercises.

#### Phase 2: Core B1 Textbook Integration (Weeks 9–18)
* **Objective:** Systematically work through a comprehensive B1 textbook series (such as *Netzwerk B1* or *Aspekte Neu*), aiming for roughly one chapter per week (10 chapters total).
* **Weekday 60-Minute Routine:**
  * 15 mins: Vocabulary review (Anki).
  * 30 mins: Reading and listening tasks directly from the textbook chapter.
  * 15 mins: Grammar exercises associated with that chapter.
* **Weekend 3-Hour Routine:**
  * **Saturday (Writing Focus):** Draft the chapter's assigned emails and opinion essays. Use tools like LanguageTool or AI to check your spelling and grammar.
  * **Sunday (Speaking & Review):** Answer B1 oral exam prompts based on the chapter's theme, record yourself to check fluency, and review all errors made during the week.

#### Phase 3: Exam-Specific Preparation & Polish (Weeks 19–26)
* **Objective:** Simulate real exam conditions, master the specific formats for *Hören, Lesen, Schreiben, Sprechen*, and fix persistent weak spots.
* **Routine:** Spend your weekday hours doing targeted mini-sections of past papers. Spend your weekend hours taking full, timed mock exams under strict test conditions, followed by rigorous self-correction.

---

### Risks and How to Mitigate Them

* **Risk of Lacking Active Speaking Feedback:** Self-study can leave a blind spot when it comes to pronunciation and spontaneous conversational output, which are vital for the B1 oral exam. 
  * *Mitigation:* Use AI voice tools (like ChatGPT voice mode) during your weekday/weekend routines to practice daily monologues and dialogues. Additionally, book 5 to 10 individual tutoring sessions on platforms like iTalki during Phase 3 to get professional feedback on your speaking and writing.
* **Risk of Burnout:** Studying 6 days a week while working full-time is demanding. 
  * *Mitigation:* Strict time-blocking is essential, but ensure you give yourself at least one full day or evening completely off to rest and prevent mental exhaustion.

## Answer B

Passing the German B1 exam in 6 months starting from A2 while working full-time is an ambitious but entirely achievable goal. 

Let's look at the math first:
* **Weekdays:** 1 hour × 5 days = 5 hours
* **Weekends:** 3 hours × 2 days = 6 hours
* **Total:** ~11 hours per week × 26 weeks = **approx. 286 hours**. 

This aligns well with the standard European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) recommendation of 100–150 hours per level, giving you plenty of time for practice and exam preparation.

Here is a structured, 6-month study plan designed for your schedule.

---

### Phase 1: Months 1 & 2 – Grammar & Vocabulary Bridge (A2 to B1)
*Goal: Close the gap between A2 and B1 by tackling core grammar and expanding your vocabulary.*

* **Monday to Friday (1 hour/day):**
  * **30 minutes:** Study grammar. Focus on B1 milestones: *Nebensätze* (subordinate clauses like *weil, dass, obwohl*), passive voice (*Passiv*), relative clauses, and subjunctive II (*Konjunktiv II*).
  * **30 minutes:** Do exercises from a B1 grammar workbook (*Übungsgrammatik*).
* **Weekend (3 hours/day):**
  * **Saturday (3 hours):** Focus on vocabulary and reading. Learn 20–30 new B1 words using spaced repetition (Anki or Quizlet). Read short, graded readers at the B1 level or simple news articles (*Nachrichten leicht*, *Deutsche Welle*).
  * **Sunday (3 hours):** Focus on listening. Listen to podcasts designed for learners (e.g., *Langsam gesprochene Nachrichten* by DW, *Coffee Break German*, or *Easy German*). Write down unfamiliar words and shadow (repeat out loud) what the speakers say.

---

### Phase 2: Months 3 & 4 – Skill Integration (Reading, Listening, Writing, Speaking)
*Goal: Stop translating in your head and start using the language actively.*

* **Monday to Friday (1 hour/day):**
  * **Day 1 (Mon): Reading.** Read a B1 text and answer comprehension questions. Underline structures you don't understand.
  * **Day 2 (Tue): Listening.** Listen to a dialogue twice without looking at the transcript, then once with the transcript. Note down useful phrases.
  * **Day 3 (Wed): Writing.** Write a short text (80–100 words)—an informal email, a formal complaint, or an opinion piece. Use a free tool like *LanguageTool* or ChatGPT to check your grammar.
  * **Day 4 (Thu): Speaking (Monologue).** Pick a B1 topic (e.g., "My last vacation," "Advantages and disadvantages of home office") and talk out loud for 2 minutes without stopping.
  * **Day 5 (Fri): Vocabulary & Review.** Review the words and grammar rules you struggled with during the week.
* **Weekend (3 hours/day):**
  * **Saturday (2 hours):** Intensive textbook study (use a complete B1 coursebook like *Schritte plus Neu B1* or *Aspekte neu*). Do the chapter exercises.
  * **Saturday (1 hour):** Watch a German TV show or YouTube channel with German subtitles (e.g., *Easy German*).
  * **Sunday (3 hours):** Language exchange. Spend 1.5 hours practicing speaking with a partner on Tandem/HelloTalk or book a 1-hour session with a tutor on iTalki to practice B1 conversation topics. Use the remaining time to review the week's mistakes.

---

### Phase 3: Months 5 & 6 – Exam Simulation & Fine-Tuning
*Goal: Master the format of the Goethe, ÖSD, or Telc B1 exam and manage your time.*

* **Monday to Friday (1 hour/day):**
  * **Mon/Tue:** Do reading and listening modules from official B1 model test books (*Modelltests*). Time yourself.
  * **Wed/Thu:** Write B1 exam essays/emails under strict time limits (e.g., 30 minutes). 
  * **Fri:** Review your mistakes. Why did you fail a listening question? Why was your grammar wrong in the writing section?
* **Weekend (3 hours/day):**
  * **Saturday (3 hours):** **Full Exam Simulation.** Once a week, sit down and do an entire exam section (or a full mock test) under exam conditions. This builds your mental stamina.
  * **Sunday (2 hours):** Speaking practice focusing specifically on the three parts of the B1 oral exam:
    1. Planning something together (*Gemeinsam etwas planen*).
    2. Presenting a topic (*Ein Thema präsentieren*).
    3. Answering questions and giving feedback.
  * **Sunday (1 hour):** Relaxed German media consumption (music, podcast) to keep stress low.

---

### 4 Golden Rules for Success with a Full-Time Job:

1. **Micro-learning on weekdays:** If you are too tired after work for a full hour, split it up. Do 20 minutes of flashcards on your commute, 20 minutes of grammar at lunchtime, and 20 minutes of listening while making dinner.
2. **Prioritize Active Output:** Many learners only read and listen (passive). To pass B1, you *must* write and speak. Don't skip the writing and speaking exercises.
3. **Get Feedback on Writing:** Don't practice writing in a vacuum. Use AI (ChatGPT) or a tutor to correct your mistakes so you don't memorize incorrect grammar.
4. **Choose Your Exam Board:** Decide early whether you are taking the **Goethe-Zertifikat B1**, **Telc B1**, or **ÖSD B1**. Buy the specific preparation books for that exam board in Month 4, as the formats differ slightly.
