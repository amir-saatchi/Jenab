[12:00:04 assistant] A moving average (MA) is a technical analysis tool that smooths out price data by creating a constantly updated average price. It helps filter out short-term "noise" (random price fluctuations) to reveal the underlying trend of an asset.

**How it works:**
It calculates the average price over a specific number of periods (e.g., 20 days, 50 days, or 200 days). As new price data becomes available, the oldest data point is dropped, and the newest one is added, causing the average to "move" along the chart.

**Common Types:**
1. **Simple Moving Average (SMA):** The arithmetic mean of the prices over the chosen period. Every day carries equal weight.
2. **Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** Gives more weight to recent prices, making it more responsive to new information and price changes.

**Common Uses:**
* **Trend Identification:** If the price is above the MA and the MA is sloping upward, the trend is generally bullish.
* **Support and Resistance:** Traders often view MAs as "dynamic" floors or ceilings where prices might bounce.
* **Crossovers:** When a short-term MA (e.g., 50-day) crosses above a long-term MA (e.g., 200-day), it is often seen as a bullish signal (known as a "Golden Cross").
