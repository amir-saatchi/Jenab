[12:00:04 assistant] A **moving average (MA)** is a statistical calculation used to smooth out price data by creating a constantly updated average price over a specific time period. It's one of the most common technical indicators in trading.

**How it works:**
- You pick a window (e.g., 20 days, 50 days, 200 days)
- For each day, you average the closing prices of the last *N* days
- As new days arrive, the oldest drops out and the newest enters — the average "moves"

**Main types:**
| Type | Formula | Characteristic |
|------|---------|----------------|
| **SMA** (Simple) | Equal weight to all *N* periods | Slower to react, smoother |
| **EMA** (Exponential) | More weight to recent prices | Faster to react, less lag |
| **WMA** (Weighted) | Linear weights, newest highest | Between SMA and EMA |

**Common uses:**
- **Trend direction**: Price above MA → uptrend; below → downtrend
- **Support/Resistance**: MAs often act as dynamic support (in uptrends) or resistance (in downtrends)
- **Crossovers**: A short MA crossing above a long MA (e.g., 50-day > 200-day = "golden cross") signals momentum shifts
- **Filtering noise**: Helps ignore day-to-day volatility

**Example:** A 20-day SMA on BTC would sum the last 20 daily closes and divide by 20. Tomorrow, drop day 1, add day 21, recalculate.
