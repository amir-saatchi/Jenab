**SMA (Simple Moving Average) vs EMA (Exponential Moving Average):**

**SMA:**
- Simple average of closing prices over a specific period
- All prices in the period have equal weight
- Formula: (Sum of closing prices) / Number of periods
- Example: 10-day SMA = (Day 1 + Day 2 + ... + Day 10) / 10

**EMA:**
- Exponential moving average that gives more weight to recent prices
- Uses a smoothing factor to emphasize recent data
- Formula: EMA = (Current Price × Smoothing Factor) + (Previous EMA × (1 - Smoothing Factor))
- Smoothing Factor = 2 / (Number of periods + 1)

**Key Differences:**
- **Weighting**: SMA treats all prices equally; EMA gives more weight to recent prices
- **Responsiveness**: EMA reacts faster to price changes than SMA
- **Lag**: SMA has more lag because it includes older data equally; EMA has less lag
- **Use cases**: SMA is better for identifying long-term trends; EMA is better for short-term trading signals
