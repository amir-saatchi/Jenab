A moving average is a widely used technical analysis tool in finance that smooths out price data by creating a constantly updated average price over a specific time period.

Here are the key points about moving averages:

**Basic Concept:**
- It calculates the average of a security's price over a set number of periods
- As new data becomes available, the oldest data point is dropped from the calculation
- This creates a "moving" average that follows price trends

**Common Types:**
- **Simple Moving Average (SMA)**: Equal weight given to all data points in the period
- **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**: Gives more weight to recent data points, making it more responsive to new information

**Common Time Periods:**
- Short-term: 10, 20, or 50 periods
- Medium-term: 100 periods
- Long-term: 200 periods

**Uses:**
- **Trend identification**: Helps determine if a security is in an uptrend (price above moving average) or downtrend (price below moving average)
- **Support/resistance levels**: Moving averages often act as dynamic support or resistance
- **Crossover signals**: When short-term moving averages cross above/below longer-term ones
- **Smoothing**: Reduces noise and highlights underlying price trends

In your crypto project, moving averages could be useful for analyzing Bitcoin price trends in your prices table.
