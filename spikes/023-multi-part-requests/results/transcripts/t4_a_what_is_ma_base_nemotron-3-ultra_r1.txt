[12:00:04 assistant] A **moving average** is a statistical calculation that smooths out price data by creating a constantly updated average price over a specific time period. It's one of the most common technical indicators in trading.

**How it works:**
- You pick a window (e.g., 20 days, 50 days, 200 days)
- For each day, you average the closing prices over that window
- As new days arrive, the oldest drops out and the newest enters — the average "moves"

**Two main types:**
| Type | Formula | Characteristic |
|------|---------|----------------|
| **SMA** (Simple Moving Average) | Equal weight to all prices in the window | Slower to react, smoother |
| **EMA** (Exponential Moving Average) | More weight to recent prices | Faster to react, less lag |

**Common uses:**
- **Trend identification**: Price above MA = uptrend; below = downtrend
- **Support/resistance**: MAs often act as dynamic support/resistance levels
- **Crossovers**: Short MA crossing above long MA = bullish signal (e.g., "golden cross" = 50-day crosses above 200-day)

**Example:** A 20-day SMA on BTC would sum the last 20 daily closes and divide by 20. Tomorrow, you'd drop day 1, add day 21, and recalculate.
