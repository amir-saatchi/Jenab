[12:00:04 assistant] A moving average (MA) is a technical analysis tool used to smooth out price data by creating a constantly updated average price. It filters out short-term "noise" (random price fluctuations) to help identify the underlying trend of an asset.

**How it works:**
It calculates the average price over a specific number of periods. As new data becomes available, the oldest data point is dropped, and the newest one is added, causing the average to "move" along the timeline.

**Common Types:**
1.  **Simple Moving Average (SMA):** The arithmetic mean of a set of prices over a specific period. (e.g., a 50-day SMA is the sum of the last 50 closing prices divided by 50).
2.  **Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** Similar to the SMA, but it gives more weight to recent prices. This makes it react faster to recent price changes.

**Common Uses:**
*   **Trend Identification:** If the price is above the MA, the trend is generally considered bullish (upward); if below, it's bearish (downward).
*   **Support and Resistance:** Traders often view MAs as "dynamic" floors or ceilings where the price might bounce.
*   **Crossovers:** When a short-term MA (e.g., 50-day) crosses above a long-term MA (e.g., 200-day), it is often seen as a bullish signal (known as a "Golden Cross").
