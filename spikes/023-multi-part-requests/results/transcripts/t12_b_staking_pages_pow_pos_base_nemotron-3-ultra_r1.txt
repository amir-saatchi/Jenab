**Proof of Work vs Proof of Stake (quick answer)**

| Aspect | Proof of Work (PoW) | Proof of Stake (PoS) |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------|
| **How blocks are produced** | Miners compete to solve a cryptographic puzzle; first to solve it proposes the next block. | Validators are chosen pseudo-randomly, weighted by the amount of ETH they’ve staked. |
| **Security resource** | Electricity + specialised hardware (ASICs/GPUs). | Capital at risk (staked ETH). |
| **Energy use** | High — Bitcoin/Ethereum (pre-Merge) consumed ~100 TWh/yr. | ~99.95% less; Ethereum post-Merge uses ~0.01 TWh/yr. |
| **Economic penalty** | Wasted electricity if you mine an invalid chain. | **Slashing** — a portion or all of your stake is burned for misbehaviour (double-signing, surround votes, inactivity). |
| **Entry barrier** | Expensive hardware, cheap electricity, physical space. | 32 ETH to run a solo validator (or less via pooling/staking services). |
| **Finality** | Probabilistic — more confirmations = more certainty. | Deterministic — epochs finalise via Casper FFG; once finalised, blocks can’t be reverted without massive slashing. |
| **Centralisation risks** | Mining pools, ASIC manufacturers, cheap-power regions. | Large staking providers (Lido, exchanges), but protocol encourages decentralisation via penalties for correlated failures. |
| **Ethereum status** | Used until **The Merge (15 Sep 2022)**. | Live since The Merge; current consensus. |

In short: PoW burns energy to make attacks expensive; PoS burns *money* (staked ETH) to make attacks expensive. Ethereum switched to PoS to cut energy use, lower hardware barriers, and enable stronger finality guarantees.
