[12:00:04 assistant] A **moving average (MA)** is a statistical calculation used to smooth out price data by creating a constantly updated average price over a specific time period. It's one of the most common technical indicators in trading.

**How it works:**
- You pick a window (e.g., 10 days, 50 days, 200 days)
- For each day, you average the closing prices over that window
- As new data comes in, the oldest drops out and the newest enters — hence "moving"

**Main types:**
| Type | Formula | Characteristic |
|------|---------|----------------|
| **SMA** (Simple) | Equal weight to all prices in the window | Slower to react |
| **EMA** (Exponential) | More weight to recent prices | Faster to react |
| **WMA** (Weighted) | Linear weighting, newest heaviest | In between |

**What traders use it for:**
- **Trend direction** — price above MA = uptrend, below = downtrend
- **Support/resistance** — MAs often act as dynamic levels
- **Crossovers** — e.g., 50-day crossing above 200-day ("golden cross") signals bullish momentum
- **Filtering noise** — smooths daily volatility to see the underlying trend

In your Crypto Watch project, you could add a moving average to the `price_chart` view (e.g., a 20-day SMA line overlaid on BTC close prices) or compute it in a pipeline for signals.
